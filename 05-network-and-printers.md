## Network, Internet & Printers

---

**BEFORE YOU SEND AN URGENT TICKET ABOUT THE INTERNET BEING 'DOWN'...**

- Check your cables, especially the power one.
- "Off and On" is not a suggestion, it's a diagnosis.
- Your local router is not our network's responsibility.

*Note: Failure to check the power plug will double your wait time.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TRY TO 'TWEAK' THE NETWORK TO MAKE IT FASTER...**

- A Mikrotik router is not a toy. If you do not know the difference between a subnet mask and a MAC address, step away from the blinking lights.
- Hitting the recessed "Reset" button with a paperclip because your Spotify wouldn't load did not "refresh the connection." It wiped a heavily customized configuration and brought the entire business to a standstill.
- We know exactly what time you decided to play Systems Administrator. The firewall logs do not lie.

*Note: Unplugging the main switch to charge your vape is grounds for immediate and permanent exile.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SUBMIT A TICKET SAYING THE PRINTER 'SUDDENLY STOPPED'...**

- Printers do not break; they negotiate terms.
- A status of "Ready" is a bold-faced lie. It is actually just evaluating its next victim.
- Printing successfully from your phone does not mean it is fixed.

*Note: Staring at the machine is not a troubleshooting step; it is a loyalty exam.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TELL ME 'THE PRINTER TOLD ME TO CHANGE THE TONER'...**

- Printers are notorious liars with a vested interest in selling you more plastic cylinders of expensive dust.
- "Low Toner" actually means "you can probably get another 400 pages out of this if you take the cartridge out and give it a good shake."
- If you replace the cartridge the literal second the warning pops up, you are throwing away company money.

*Note: Anyone caught replacing a toner cartridge that still has weight to it will be forced to manually transcribe their documents with a ballpoint pen.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BLAME THE 'VPN' FOR YOUR INABILITY TO WORK...**

- My Tailscale configuration is routing perfectly. The network is stable, secure, and fully operational.
- The reason you cannot reach the company server is because you are trying to work from a coffee shop whose public Wi-Fi actively blocks everything except web browsing.
- A secure tunnel cannot physically push data through a firewall built by a barista.

*Note: Submitting a high-priority ticket because your favorite café has bad internet is an abuse of the support desk. Buy a better data plan.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TRY TO 'UPGRADE' THE OFFICE HARDWARE...**

- RAM is slotted. CPUs are gently socketed. If you have to push down so hard that the motherboard visibly bends, you are committing a hardware felony.
- Splicing random wires into the power supply because you watched a tutorial on custom ARGB LED strips is how you burn down the accounting department.
- A cheap soldering iron and blind confidence are the most terrifying combination known to IT.

*Note: We can literally smell the burnt silicon from down the hall. Your workstation is now a crime scene.*

**The Printer: A Technical Poem**

*And that's when IT leans back in the chair… not sitting anymore, but spiritually collapsing into the swivel mechanism.*

*Because now the truth reveals itself: The printer was never broken. It was never fixed. It was negotiating terms.*

You clear the queue. Nothing prints. You delete the printer. It reappears. You uninstall drivers. Windows says: "Don't worry, I kept a copy."

The network printers arrive out of nowhere, like they were summoned. "HP LaserJet in Accounting," "Canon Printer in a different building you've never visited," "Brother Printer (Shared via hope and Wi-Fi prayers)." None of them work. All of them are available. Like abandoned taxis in a ghost city.

You try IP printing. Ping works. Port 9100? Dead silence. SNMP? Laughs at you in binary. Web interface? Last updated: 2014. Login: admin / admin. Password: "changed" (it has not been changed).

Meanwhile the user is standing there saying: "It worked yesterday."

So you reboot everything. PC. Router. Printer. Emotionally yourself. Printer wakes up. Prints… half a page. Then stops. Like it got tired of your expectations.

Then Windows Print Spooler crashes. Silently. Gracefully. Like a butler quitting mid-shift without telling anyone. Restart service. It starts. Immediately stops. Like it saw the printer and reconsidered life choices.

Printer status changes from "Offline" to: "Error" / "Driver unavailable" / "Attention required" / "Need user intervention" (no explanation) / "Ready" (bold-faced lie).

It becomes a mood ring for suffering.

Print from phone: Ding. Printed. Perfect. Clean. Like nothing ever went wrong. The printer didn't fix itself. It simply chose a different victim.

*At this point, IT stares at the printer and understands: This is not a device. It is a personality test. A loyalty exam. A ritual gatekeeper between humans and paper-based reality.*
