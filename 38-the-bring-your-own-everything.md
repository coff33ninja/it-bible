## The "Bring Your Own Everything"

---

**BEFORE YOU PLUG YOUR PERSONAL ROUTER INTO THE COMPANY NETWORK...**

- You brought a R149 router from home, plugged it into the wall port in your office, and created a rogue DHCP server that is now handing out IP addresses to everyone within range. The network is chaos. You are the reason.
- "The Wi-Fi was slow so I brought my own" — the Wi-Fi was slow because of network congestion, which you have now made exponentially worse by adding an unmanaged, consumer-grade access point to the enterprise infrastructure.
- That rogue router has no security configuration, no VLAN segregation, and no password policy. Anyone within range can now access your "personal" network, which is bridged to the corporate network. You have created a backdoor.

*Note: Plugging a personal router into the corporate network is not "being resourceful." It is "creating a security incident." Unplug it immediately.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CONNECT YOUR PERSONAL LAPTOP TO THE COMPANY NETWORK...**

- Your personal laptop is not managed by IT. It has no antivirus, no encryption, no patch management, and no security policy enforcement. It is an unknown variable connected to a controlled environment.
- "I just needed to check my email" — check it on your phone. Do not bridge your unsecured personal device into the corporate network because you are too lazy to pull out your phone.
- Your personal laptop has been used to download torrents, visit questionable websites, and share files with people who are not subject to our security policies. It is a liability. It does not belong on our network.

*Note: Your personal laptop is not welcome on the corporate network. It is not about trust. It is about the 47 things on that machine that you do not know are there.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING YOUR OWN MONITOR FROM HOME...**

- That monitor from home has VGA input only. Your company laptop does not have VGA. You will need an adapter. You do not have an adapter. You expect IT to provide one.
- "It is a perfectly good monitor" — it is a 720p panel from 2012 with a cracked bezel, a stuck pixel in the centre, and colour calibration that makes everything look sepia-toned.
- The company provides monitors for a reason. They are standardised, tested, and supported. Your home monitor is not. When it fails, you will blame IT for the poor display quality.

*Note: Bringing your own monitor from home is not "saving the company money." It is "introducing an unsupported variable into the standardised setup."*

**BEFORE YOU BRING YOUR PERSONAL PRINTER AND INSTALL IT...**

- You bought a R399 printer from a supermarket, brought it to the office, and connected it to your workstation via USB. The drivers were downloaded from a third-party site because the manufacturer no longer hosts them.
- "It was cheaper than using the office printer" — it is cheaper per page because you are not factoring in the cost of your time, the IT support time, and the consumables you will need to buy yourself.
- Your personal printer is now everyone's problem. When it jams, you will ask IT to fix it. When the toner runs out, you will ask IT to order it. When it breaks, you will expect IT to replace it.

*Note: A personal printer brought into the office is not a "cost-saving measure." It is a support liability that you just handed to the IT department.*

**BEFORE YOU INSTALL YOUR OWN SOFTWARE ON THE COMPANY LAPTOP...**

- You downloaded "Free Video Player Pro" from a pop-up ad and installed it. It came with 3 browser toolbars, a search engine hijacker, and a crypto miner. Your machine is now part of a botnet.
- "It was free and I needed it" — the cost was your machine's security. The "free" software was monetised by bundling malware. You paid the price without realising it.
- Installation rights are restricted for a reason. Every piece of software on your company laptop has been vetted for security, compatibility, and licensing. Your unauthorised install bypasses all of that.

*Note: Installing unauthorised software on a company device is not "taking initiative." It is "violating the acceptable use policy." The software will be removed. Your machine will be scanned.*
