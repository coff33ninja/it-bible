## The Meeting That Could Have Been an Email

---

**BEFORE YOU SCHEDULE A 1-HOUR MEETING TO READ A SPREADSHEET...**

- You invited 14 people to a 1-hour meeting to read a spreadsheet out loud. The spreadsheet was sent as an attachment in the invitation. Everyone could have read it in 5 minutes at their desk.
- 14 people × 60 minutes = 14 person-hours spent on something that could have been accomplished with a 5-minute email. That is 13 hours and 55 minutes of collective life wasted.
- "I wanted to discuss it" — discuss what? The numbers are on the page. The trends are visible. The decisions can be made via email. You called a meeting because you did not want to write an email.

*Note: A meeting where one person talks and everyone else listens is called a "presentation." Those can be recorded and watched at 1.5x speed. Do not book my calendar for a monologue.*

---

**BEFORE YOU INVITE THE ENTIRE COMPANY TO A STATUS UPDATE...**

- A status update for the 3-person project team does not need to include the CEO, the board, the entire marketing department, and that intern from accounting who has never heard of this project.
- "Just in case they are interested" — if they are interested, they will read the meeting notes. You do not need to occupy their calendar for an hour to keep them "in the loop."
- Every person in that room is billing their time to a department. Your meeting is costing the company thousands of rands per hour. For a status update that could have been a bullet-point list.

*Note: "Covering all bases" is not a justification for calendar terrorism.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BOOK A MEETING WITHOUT AN AGENDA...**

- A meeting invitation with no agenda, no context, and a title like "Quick catch-up" is a black hole of productivity. Nobody knows what to prepare, what to bring, or why they are there.
- "We will figure it out when we get there" — no. We will spend the first 15 minutes figuring out what the meeting is about, then rush through the actual content, then schedule a follow-up because we ran out of time.
- An agenda is not optional. It is the minimum viable requirement for any meeting. If you cannot write 3 bullet points about what the meeting covers, you are not ready to hold it.

*Note: A meeting without an agenda is a social gathering. Schedule it after hours if you want to socialise.*

---

**BEFORE YOU START THE MEETING WITH 'LET'S GO AROUND THE ROOM AND INTRODUCE OURSELVES'...**

- There are 18 people in this meeting. You work in the same company. Some of you sit 3 desks apart. You do not need introductions. You need to respect everyone's time.
- "I want everyone to feel included" — everyone will feel included when the meeting ends on time and they can get back to work. Not when they have to listen to 18 people recite their job titles.
- If you do not know who is in the room, check the calendar invitation. You sent it. You should know who you invited.

*Note: Round-robin introductions in a meeting of more than 6 people is a hostage situation, not team building.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BOOK A FOLLOW-UP MEETING BECAUSE THE FIRST ONE RAN OVER...**

- The first meeting ran over because there was no agenda, no timekeeping, and no decision-making. The follow-up will run over for the same reasons.
- You are not solving the problem by booking more time. You are compounding the problem by doubling the wasted time.
- If the first meeting did not achieve its objective, the solution is not a second meeting. The solution is a clear agenda, pre-reading materials, and a strict time limit for the first meeting.

*Note: A follow-up meeting to "continue the discussion" is an admission that the first meeting was a failure. Fix the process, not the calendar.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BOOK A MEETING FOR 'SYNCING UP'...**

- "Let's sync up" is the vaguest meeting title in corporate history. It communicates nothing about the purpose, the expected outcome, or what attendees need to prepare.
- Sync up on what? The project status? The roadmap? The coffee machine maintenance schedule? I cannot prepare for a meeting if I do not know what it is about.
- "Sync" implies two-way communication. If you are going to talk at me for 45 minutes with 5 minutes for questions, that is not a sync. That is a broadcast.

*Note: "Sync up" is the corporate equivalent of "we need to talk." It fills everyone with dread and accomplishes nothing.*

---

**BEFORE YOU RECORD THE MEETING 'FOR THOSE WHO COULD NOT ATTEND'...**

- The meeting could have been an email. Now it is a recorded meeting that nobody will watch because it is 60 minutes long and could have been summarised in 3 bullet points.
- "I will send the recording afterwards" — you will not. But even if you do, nobody will watch it. They will skim the transcript if one exists, or they will ask you in Slack what was decided.
- The recording is not a substitute for proper documentation. Write down the decisions, action items, and next steps. That is what people need, not a video of 14 people staring at a spreadsheet.

*Note: A recorded meeting is not documentation. It is digital archaeology. Future generations will not excavate your Teams recording to find out why the budget was approved.*
