## Cable Management Crimes

---

**BEFORE YOU SHOW ME YOUR 'CABLE MANAGEMENT'...**

- A tangled nest of cables shoved behind the desk is not "managed." It is an electrified bird's nest waiting to short-circuit.
- If I cannot identify which cable belongs to which device without tracing it with my finger like a detective solving a murder, you have failed.
- Velcro straps exist. Cable ties exist. Labelling exists. You chose chaos.

*Note: "I'll sort it out later" — later is now. I am here. Sort it out while I watch.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CRIMP YOUR OWN ETHERNET CABLE...**

- That RJ45 connector you terminated has the wires in approximately the right order but the colours are in a sequence that does not appear in any known standard.
- The crimp is loose. The jacket is not clamped. The exposed copper is longer than the insulation. This cable will work exactly until you need it most, then fail silently.
- A factory-made patch cable costs R50. Your "homemade" cable will cost me two hours of my life diagnosing intermittent connectivity.

*Note: Unless you are a certified network cabling technician, leave the crimping tool in the drawer. You are not saving money; you are creating future suffering.*

---

**BEFORE YOU PLUG A POWER STRIP INTO ANOTHER POWER STRIP...**

- Daisy-chaining power strips is not "extending your reach." It is a fire hazard with extra steps.
- The maximum load rating does not multiply because you plugged one strip into another. It divides. You are now operating dangerously close to thermal event territory.
- Extension cords under carpets, power strips behind desks where they cannot be unplugged, and cables stretched taut across walkways are not "temporary solutions." They are OSHA violation exhibits waiting to happen.

*Note: A daisy-chained power strip does not give you more power. It gives the fire department more work.*

---

**BEFORE YOU RUN CABLE THROUGH THE CEILING TILES...**

- The ceiling is not a cable tray. Dropping unsecured, unrated Ethernet cable across the suspended ceiling grid is how you interfere with fire suppression sprinklers and emergency lighting.
- Yes, it looks clean from below. Yes, it is a violation of every building code written since 1975.
- If the ceiling tiles have to be removed for maintenance and your unmarked cable comes crashing down, I am not responsible for identifying which of the 14 unlabeled cables belongs to your department.

*Note: Hidden cable is not "clean." It is "hidden." There is a difference.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE ZIP TIES LIKE A WEAPON...**

- Zip ties are for temporary bundling, not for cinching cables so tight that the inner conductors are permanently deformed.
- A zip tie tightened with the force of a thousand suns does not improve cable management. It creates a point of failure where the cable will eventually break internally.
- Also, do not zip-tie cables to structural pipes, gas lines, or sprinkler systems. If I have to explain why that is wrong, you should not be touching cables.

*Note: Snip the zip tie flush with the head so the next person does not draw blood retrieving it. Basic human decency.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLAIM 'THE CABLE WAS LIKE THAT WHEN I GOT HERE'...**

- The cable has been there for seven years. You have been in this office for six of those years. You are not an innocent bystander; you are an accessory.
- Cables do not naturally migrate, tangle themselves, or accumulate dust bunnies the size of small mammals. That is neglect.
- Every time you plug and unplug a USB cable by yanking the cord instead of the connector, you shorten its lifespan. One day it will stop working. That day is your fault.

*Note: Cable entropy is not a law of physics. It is a law of laziness.*
