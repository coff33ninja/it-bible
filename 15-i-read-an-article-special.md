## The "I Read an Article" Special

---

**BEFORE YOU TELL ME YOUR 'CYBERSECURITY RESEARCH'...**

- Reading one article about the latest zero-day vulnerability does not make you a cybersecurity analyst. It makes you informed. There is a difference.
- No, that vulnerability does not apply to our environment. We patched it three months ago. You are the last person to hear about it.
- Forwarding a sensationalist news headline to the entire company with "ARE WE SAFE?!" is not security awareness. It is panic marketing.

*Note: If the article came from a pop-up ad, it is not journalism. It is malware advertising disguised as a warning.*

---

**BEFORE YOU DEMAND AN 'IMMEDIATE SECURITY REVIEW' AFTER ONE NEWS STORY...**

- That single Bloomberg article about a new attack vector did not instantly invalidate our entire security posture.
- Our firewall rules, endpoint detection, and patch management pipeline were not designed by people who read one article.
- If you are suddenly concerned about security, start by not using "Password123" for your corporate login.

*Note: A sudden interest in cybersecurity because you watched a Netflix documentary does not grant you override authority over the IT security policy.*

---

**BEFORE YOU FOLLOW ADVICE FROM A 'TECH BLOG' YOU FOUND ON FACEBOOK...**

- That article recommending you "disable Windows Defender for better performance" was written by someone who does not understand security.
- "10 Registry Hacks to Speed Up Your PC" is how you end up with a machine that cannot boot.
- No, deleting System32 will not "free up valuable disk space." It will destroy your operating system. This is not a prank. It is reality.

*Note: If the article is hosted on a site that also sells weight loss gummies, perhaps do not apply its technical advice to your work machine.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLAIM 'THE INTERNET SAID THIS IS THE BEST ANTIVIRUS'...**

- The "Top 10 Antivirus Software" list you read was a paid advertisement. Every single entry paid to be on that list.
- The best antivirus is the one centrally managed by the IT department and pushed to your machine automatically.
- Installing a second, third, or fourth antivirus "just to be safe" actually makes you less safe. They conflict, crash, and leave gaps.

*Note: If your antivirus was advertised during a YouTube preroll, it is probably not the best option.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE A 'PRIVACY TIP' FROM A FORUM...**

- Some random Reddit user's advice to "disable all telemetry via a script from this pastebin" is not a privacy strategy. It is an infection vector.
- "I found this tool that blocks all Microsoft tracking" — and that tool also blocks your ability to receive security updates. Congratulations.
- Forum advice is not vetted. It is not peer-reviewed. It is someone's opinion typed into a text box at 2 AM.

*Note: No, you cannot name a child "Null" to bypass login screens. That stopped working a decade ago. Stop trying.*

---

**BEFORE YOU PANIC ABOUT A 'CRITICAL ALERT' POP-UP...**

- "Your computer is infected! Call this number immediately!" is a scam. A real antivirus does not demand you call a toll-free number.
- Full-screen browser pop-ups that mimic Windows system alerts are not legitimate. They are social engineering.
- The "Your license has expired!" notification that asks for your credit card to renew? Not from us. We do not charge you to use the company laptop.

*Note: If the alert asks for payment in iTunes gift cards, it is not a security warning. It is a comedy of errors waiting to happen.*

---

**BEFORE YOU QUOTE AN ARTICLE ABOUT HOW 'WI-FI CAUSES HEALTH ISSUES'...**

- That study was debunked, retracted, and the author was found to have fabricated data. It is not a credible source.
- Wi-Fi uses non-ionizing radiation. It does not cause cancer, brain damage, or attract insects. It just moves data through the air.
- If you are genuinely concerned, the solution is not to demand we disable the Wi-Fi for the entire office. It is to educate yourself on basic physics.

*Note: The electromagnetic field from your Wi-Fi router is weaker than the natural background radiation you are exposed to during a single flight to Cape Town.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING UP 'THAT ARTICLE ABOUT THE GOOGLE EMPLOYEE MONITORING'...**

- Yes, major tech companies collect data. This has been known since the early 2000s. It is not a breaking news revelation.
- No, they are not listening to your private conversations through your phone's microphone to serve you ads. That would be illegal, impractical, and unnecessary. They already have enough data from your search history.
- Sharing a sensationalist headline from 2018 as if it just dropped today does not make you a privacy advocate. It makes you late to the conversation.

*Note: If you are truly concerned about privacy, start by reading the terms of service for the apps you already installed. The answer is always in the fine print.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TELL ME 'A FRIEND OF A FRIEND SAID...'**

- Secondhand technical advice from someone who knows someone who "works in IT" is not a reliable source.
- "My cousin's roommate fixed the same issue by deleting this file" — and your cousin's roommate's fix is why your machine is now also broken.
- Referral advice is not documentation. If it cannot be verified by a reputable source, it does not belong on a company device.

*Note: The friend of a friend who "works in IT" may be the same person who thinks the "any key" is a real button on the keyboard.*
