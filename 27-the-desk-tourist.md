## The Desk Tourist

---

**BEFORE YOU STAND BEHIND SOMEONE WHILE THEY WORK...**

- Standing directly behind a colleague and breathing down their neck while they troubleshoot does not make the computer work faster. It makes the person work slower.
- "Is it fixed yet?" asked every 45 seconds for 15 minutes does not constitute technical support. It constitutes psychological warfare.
- The machine does not respond to audience pressure. It does not perform better under observation. Your presence is not helping.

*Note: Watching someone type is not a team sport. Go back to your desk. We will call you when it is done.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING YOUR LAPTOP TO SOMEONE'S DESK AND SAY 'JUST LOOK AT THIS QUICKLY'...**

- "Quickly" is the longest unit of time in IT support. It starts as 30 seconds and expands to fill the entire afternoon.
- You have walked across the office, interrupted someone mid-task, and now expect them to drop everything because you did not submit a ticket.
- The issue you are about to describe has been happening for 3 weeks. It is not urgent. You just decided now is the time to deal with it — which happens to be when the technician is busy.

*Note: A physical ambush at someone's desk is not a support channel. Submit a ticket like a civilised person.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CALL OUT 'HEY IT GUY!' FROM ACROSS THE ROOM...**

- I have a name. It is on my email signature. It is on my office door. It is on the company directory. Use it.
- Shouting "Hey IT!" across an open-plan office while 30 people turn around to see who is summoning me like a summoned demon is disrespectful to everyone involved.
- "Hey, while you are here..." — I am not "here." I was walking to the bathroom. I was going home. I was in the middle of something. You do not get to hijack my trajectory.

*Note: If you see me in the hallway, the cafeteria, or the parking lot, that is not an invitation to open a support ticket.*

---

**BEFORE YOU KNOCK ON THE IT ROOM DOOR REPEATEDLY...**

- The IT room door is closed because I am concentrating on something that requires focus. Knocking every 30 seconds does not make me finish faster.
- "I know the door is closed but this is quick" — the door is closed. That is a sign. It means "do not disturb." It does not mean "knock until someone answers."
- The number of times you knock is inversely proportional to how helpful I will be when I finally open the door.

*Note: A closed door means "I am busy." A locked door means "I am very busy and I do not trust you." A door with a sign that says "Do Not Disturb" means I have already guessed you would ignore it.*

---

**BEFORE YOU WATCH OVER MY SHOULDER WHILE I WORK...**

- Reading my screen over my shoulder is not collaboration. It is surveillance. I do not come to your desk and read your email over your shoulder.
- "I am just interested in what you are doing" — I am terminating a process. It looks exactly like a black screen with white text. There is nothing interesting here. Go away.
- Every time I sense someone watching my screen, I make an error. That error costs time. That time is billable to your department.

*Note: The window into my soul is behind my eyes, not on my monitor. Personal space applies to screens too.*
