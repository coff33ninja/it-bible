## The Phishing Forwarder

---

**BEFORE YOU FORWARD THAT 'SCAM WARNING' TO THE ENTIRE COMPANY...**

- You are not a cybersecurity researcher. You are not doing us a favour. You are amplifying spam to 200 people who did not ask for it.
- The "scam" you forwarded went to your entire address book including the CEO, the board, and that intern who started yesterday. They all now think you are a security risk.
- If the email was truly dangerous, the security team already knows about it. They have a system. You are not part of it.

*Note: Forwarding a phishing email to "everyone" does not make you a hero. It makes you the reason we have to send a "please ignore the previous email" follow-up.*

---

**BEFORE YOU REPLY-ALL TO A PHISHING TEST...**

- The IT department sent a fake phishing email to test your awareness. You not only clicked the link, but you also replied-all to the entire distribution list asking "Is this legitimate?"
- You have now announced to 500 people that you failed the test. HR has a copy of the reply-all. Your manager has been notified.
- The reply-all button is the most dangerous button in corporate email. Do not touch it. Ever. Under any circumstances. Pretend it does not exist.

*Note: Replying-all to a phishing test is how you secure a mandatory re-training session. Congratulations.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLICK 'YOU WON AN IPHONE' AT 3 AM...**

- You did not win an iPhone. You have never won an iPhone. You will never win an iPhone from a pop-up ad on a streaming site.
- The SMS that says "Your parcel is delayed" with a link that goes to a domain like "dhl-update-verification.xyz" is not from DHL. It is from a guy in a basement.
- Clicking the link at 3 AM while half-asleep and entering your work email and password is not a mistake. It is a catastrophic lapse in judgment that will result in a compromised account.

*Note: If it sounds too good to be true, it is a phishing attempt. If it sounds urgent and scary, it is also a phishing attempt. If it asks for your password, it is definitely a phishing attempt.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CALL IT IN A PANIC AFTER CLICKING THE LINK...**

- The first thing you should do is disconnect your machine from the network. Do not call IT first. Disconnect first. Call second.
- Yes, we know you clicked it. The security logs already flagged it. We were about to call you.
- "I clicked it but I did not enter anything" — good. That is the best-case scenario. Still disconnect. Still report it. Still accept that we are going to run a full scan on your machine.

*Note: Calling IT in a panic is fine. Calling IT after clicking the link and then doing nothing for 20 minutes while the payload installs is less fine.*

---

**BEFORE YOU REUSE YOUR WORK PASSWORD FOR PERSONAL SITES...**

- That recipe website you signed up for in 2019 had a data breach. Your email and password are now on the dark web. Someone is currently trying that same password on your corporate email.
- Password reuse is the single biggest security vulnerability in any organisation. Not the firewall. Not the antivirus. Your recycling of the same password across 47 different websites.
- "But it is a strong password!" — it does not matter how strong it is if it was leaked in a breach and is now available for anyone to download.

*Note: A password manager costs nothing. Reused passwords cost everything. Use unique passwords for every service. This is not optional.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLAIM 'I DID NOT CLICK ANYTHING'...**

- The security logs say you clicked. The timestamp matches your login session. The IP address matches your machine. You clicked.
- "I do not remember clicking" — that is called autopilot. You were browsing without thinking. Your muscle memory betrayed you. It happens. But lying about it means I cannot help you.
- If you admit you clicked, I can run the remediation and have you back online in 30 minutes. If you deny it, I have to treat your machine as fully compromised and rebuild it from scratch.

*Note: "I did not click anything" is the most expensive lie in IT security. The truth is cheaper and faster.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'WHY DO WE NEED ALL THESE SECURITY PROMPTS?'...**

- Every security prompt exists because someone before you did something catastrophic that required a new prompt to prevent.
- MFA is not optional. It is the only thing standing between your email and a teenager in Lagos who wants to send spam from your account.
- "But it takes an extra 10 seconds!" — restoring your identity after a breach takes 10 weeks. Choose which 10 you prefer to spend.

*Note: Security is inconvenient by design. If it were convenient, it would not be secure.*
