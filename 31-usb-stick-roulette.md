## USB Stick Roulette

---

**BEFORE YOU PLUG THAT MYSTERY USB STICK INTO YOUR WORK COMPUTER...**

- You found a USB stick in the parking lot and your first instinct was to plug it into the work PC. That is not curiosity. That is a digital suicide wish.
- The USB stick from the conference swag bag went straight into your laptop without a second thought. You do not know who handled that stick before you. You do not know what else is on it.
- "It was in my drawer for three years, it's probably fine." Three years is not a sterilization period. Malware does not have an expiration date.
- The "I found it in the break room" defense does not hold up when the entire network goes down.
- You picked up a USB stick from the parking lot and plugged it in because you thought it might contain "interesting files." It did contain files. They were encrypting your hard drive.
- You found a USB stick labeled "Salary Data 2025" and plugged it in immediately. It was not salary data. It was a keystroke logger.
- The USB stick was shaped like a rubber duck. That is not cute. That is a Rubber Ducky attack. It is typing commands as fast as the human thumb can manage — except it is not a human thumb.
- You saw a USB stick on the floor and thought "free storage." It was free storage the same way a bear trap is free footwear.
- The colleague who said "I ran a virus scan on it" ran Windows Defender once in 2019. That scan result is not valid.
- You collected USB sticks from the lost-and-found bin and tested each one "just to see." You saw the inside of a ransomware screen.
- "I only opened one file." That is how it starts. Patient zero never says "I opened all of them."
- You tried to charge your phone using a USB port on a public kiosk. That is called juice jacking. Your phone is now exfiltrating data to a stranger.
- The USB stick was left on your desk with a note that said "urgent." Urgent is a red flag. Real urgent documents are sent via email with a proper chain of custody.
- You bought a cheap USB hub from a random vendor at a tech flea market. The hub contains a hidden chip that logs every keystroke you make through it.
- The USB stick came free with a subscription box. Free hardware is never free. The cost is your network integrity.
- You plugged in a USB stick from a vendor at a trade show without asking IT to vet it first. That vendor's logo does not make it safe.
- "I formatted it first." Formatting does not fix hardware-level exploits. The controller chip can be reprogrammed to inject malware on format.
- You found a microSD card on the sidewalk and used it in your dashcam. That microSD now has remote access to your home network.
- The USB stick was given to you by a user who said "it has a virus, can you fix it?" and you plugged it into your machine instead of an isolated sandbox.
- You still have the USB stick from your previous company and you plugged it into your new work computer. That is how cross-contamination works in the digital world.
- You thought BadUSB was a myth. It is not a myth. It is a proven attack vector and you just inserted patient zero into the company network.
- You plugged a USB stick into the server because "it needed a driver." Servers do not need drivers from unidentified USB sticks. Servers need isolation.
- The USB stick was attached to a keychain that also had a company badge. That badge now looks like an endorsement. It is not.

**Remember:** Treat every unknown USB stick like it is coated in biological hazard. Because in cybersecurity terms, it is.
