## The Password Resetter

---

**BEFORE YOU RESET YOUR PASSWORD AND FORGET IT IMMEDIATELY...**

- You just changed your password 30 seconds ago. You wrote it on a sticky note. You stuck the sticky note to your monitor. You then closed the change-password window and immediately forgot what you typed.
- "I do not remember my new password" — you set it. You confirmed it. You watched the confirmation message. The password is somewhere between your short-term and long-term memory, lost in transit.
- This is the third time this month. The password policy has not changed. The process has not changed. Your approach to remembering things has not changed either, apparently.

*Note: A password reset that you forget within 60 seconds is not a technical issue. It is a cognitive one. Write it down somewhere that is not your hand.*

---

**BEFORE YOU RESET YOUR PASSWORD AGAIN 'JUST TO BE SAFE'...**

- You did not need to reset your password. Your password was not compromised. The system did not prompt you to reset it. You "just felt like it" and now you cannot log in.
- Every reset increases the chance that you will lock yourself out, forget the new password, or write it down somewhere insecure.
- "I thought it was a good idea" — it was not. The password was working fine. You fixed something that was not broken, and now you have broken it.

*Note: Proactive password changes without being prompted are not security best practice. They are self-inflicted wounds.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE THE SAME PASSWORD WITH ONE CHARACTER CHANGED...**

- `Password1` → `Password2` → `Password3` does not fool anyone. The system remembers. The password history check exists specifically to catch this.
- "I just add a number at the end" — yes. Everyone does. That is why password history checks look for exactly that pattern.
- If I can guess your new password based on your last 5 passwords, so can an attacker with a basic script.

*Note: Rotating through `Spring2024`, `Summer2024`, `Autumn2024` is not password security. It is a calendar with letters.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CALL IT TO UNLOCK YOUR ACCOUNT FOR THE 5TH TIME THIS MONTH...**

- Your account is locked because you entered the wrong password too many times. It is locked because you kept guessing. You guessed wrong 5 times in a row.
- "I am sure it is the right password" — it is not. The system is sure it is not. The system is never wrong about this. You are.
- At some point, the definition of insanity doing the same thing and expecting different results applies directly to your password entry behaviour.

*Note: We have a self-service unlock portal. It sends a code to your phone. If you cannot remember your password and cannot use the portal, we have reached a new level of helplessness.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK WHY YOUR NEW PASSWORD 'IS NOT WORKING'...**

- Caps Lock is on. It is always on. I have never encountered a user who checked Caps Lock before calling IT.
- You are typing your password into the "Username" field. The system is rejecting it because "Administrator" is not a valid password. That is correct behaviour.
- You changed your password 5 minutes ago and are now trying to log in with your old password. The old password was replaced. It does not work anymore.

*Note: 90% of "my password does not work" calls are solved by turning off Caps Lock. 90% of the remaining 10% are solved by reading the screen. Read the screen before calling.*

---

**BEFORE YOU WRITE YOUR PASSWORD ON A STICKY NOTE...**

- A sticky note on your monitor is visible to everyone who walks past your desk, including the cleaning staff, visitors, and the person who will later use it to send emails from your account.
- "I trust everyone in the office" — security is not about trust. It is about preventing the one person who should not have access from having access.
- If you need to write your password down, use a password manager. If you refuse to use a password manager, at least put the sticky note inside your drawer, not on your screen.

*Note: A password on a sticky note stuck to the monitor is not a "system." It is a "security incident waiting to happen."*
