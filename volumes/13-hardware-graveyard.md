## The Hardware Graveyard

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING ME A DRIVE THAT'S BEEN 'MAKING A FUNNY NOISE FOR MONTHS'...**

- That "funny noise" is the sound of the read/write head physically scraping across the platter. It is not a quirky personality trait of your hard drive.
- A clicking hard drive is not "still working fine." It is performing its death rattle.
- You had months to back up your data. You chose not to. Now the drive is beyond recovery without spending more than the data is worth.

*Note: No, we cannot "just glue the platter back together." That is not how any of this works.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK WHY THE 'OLD SPARE' PC WON'T BOOT...**

- That machine has been sitting in a damp store room for six years. The CMOS battery has expired, the thermal paste is the consistency of chalk, and the PSU capacitors are bulging like they're about to give birth.
- "It was working fine when we put it away" — yes, and a car left in a field for a decade was "driving fine" when it was parked.
- You cannot deploy a 15-year-old Core 2 Duo with 2GB of DDR2 RAM and expect it to run Windows 11. You cannot even expect it to run a calculator without audible suffering.

*Note: The "spare" PC is not a spare. It is an archaeological artifact.*

---

**BEFORE YOU COMPLAIN THAT THE 'NEW' SECOND-HAND SERVER IS LOUD...**

- Enterprise server fans are not designed to be quiet. They are designed to move enough air to cool a small furnace.
- That Delta fan screaming at 10,000 RPM is not broken. It is working exactly as intended. Your expectations are what is broken.
- A server rack belongs in a soundproofed server room, not wedged under someone's desk in the open-plan office.

*Note: If you wanted silent, you should have bought a laptop. If you wanted enterprise compute, you accept the jet engine noises.*

---

**BEFORE YOU HAND ME A USB DRIVE THAT HAS BEEN THROUGH WAR...**

- This USB drive has lived in your pocket with loose change, been washed in a jeans cycle, dipped in coffee, and used as a keychain for three years.
- It has been plugged into 47 different machines across 4 continents, accumulating malware like a digital venereal disease.
- "All my important files are on here, it's my only copy" — then why is it treated like a lost luggage tag?

*Note: A USB drive is temporary storage, not a permanent backup solution. Especially not one held together by sheer hope and corrosion.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING IN A POWER SUPPLY THAT 'POPPED'...**

- That loud bang and magic blue smoke was the PSU sacrificing itself to protect your components. Be grateful, not annoyed.
- No, we cannot "just fix the blown capacitor." A PSU that failed once will fail again, and next time it might take the entire motherboard with it.
- Buying the cheapest no-name PSU on Takealot because "it's just power" is how you turn your entire PC into a paperweight.

*Note: A PSU is not a component you cheap out on. It is the single point of failure for your entire machine. Respect the power supply.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK TO RECOVER DATA FROM A DRIVE THAT WAS REFORMATTED...**

- "I accidentally formatted the wrong drive" is a sentence that should never be uttered by anyone who values their data.
- The more you use the machine after formatting, the more of the old data gets overwritten. Every second counts.
- No, running a quick format and then installing a fresh OS on top does not make recovery "easier." It makes it nearly impossible.

*Note: Data recovery is expensive, time-consuming, and never guaranteed. The only "recovery" strategy that works is having a backup before you break it.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BLAME THE 'POWER SURGE' FOR EVERYTHING...**

- A proper surge protector costs less than a pizza. If your equipment is plugged directly into the wall, you are gambling with fire.
- Yes, a surge can kill a device instantly. No, that does not explain why the screen is cracked.
- Lightning strikes are rare. Dropping your laptop off your desk is not. Stop blaming the weather.

*Note: If every single one of your devices dies a "mysterious electrical death" while your neighbor's identical setup is fine, check your own wiring before calling IT.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK TO REPURPOSE THE 'OFFICE PRINTER' FROM 2005...**

- That printer connects via parallel port. Your modern laptop does not have a parallel port. An adapter exists, but it will not make the printer work.
- The drivers were last updated for Windows XP. They do not exist for Windows 11. They will never exist.
- "Can't you just make it work?" — I can, by setting up a dedicated Windows XP VM with a passthrough parallel port, and you will not like the hourly rate for that configuration.

*Note: Some hardware belongs in a museum, not on your network. Let it go.*
