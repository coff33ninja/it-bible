## Cloud / SaaS Confusion

---

**BEFORE YOU BLAME 'THE CLOUD' FOR YOUR MISSING FILES...**

- "The Cloud" is not a single magical place where all data floats eternally. It is someone else's server in a data centre in a specific country with a specific IP address.
- If you saved your file to your desktop and then tell me "it should be in the cloud because we have OneDrive," you do not understand how OneDrive works. It syncs from specific folders. Your desktop may or may not be one of them.
- "The Cloud lost my file" — the cloud did not lose it. You saved it somewhere you cannot find it. Search harder before submitting the ticket.

*Note: The Cloud is not a backup strategy. It is a sync strategy. If you deleted it locally, it deleted from the cloud too. That is working as designed.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SAY 'THE CLOUD IS DOWN'...**

- "The cloud" as a whole entity is not down. That would mean Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and every other provider simultaneously ceased to exist. That did not happen.
- Your specific SaaS application may be experiencing an outage. That is not "the cloud." That is "the app." Two different things.
- Before reporting "the cloud is down," check: Is your internet working? Can you open other websites? Have you tried turning it off and on again? If the answer to any of these is "no," the cloud is fine.

*Note: "The cloud is down" is almost always "my internet is down" dressed up in fancier clothes.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SIGN UP FOR A SAAS PRODUCT WITHOUT TELLING IT...**

- That project management tool you found, signed up for with your work email, and started using without going through procurement or security review is now a Shadow IT liability.
- You agreed to terms of service that I have not reviewed. You stored company data on a server I do not control. You created a password that will be reused and leaked in the next data breach.
- "But it was free!" — nothing is free. The product costs either your data, your privacy, or your compliance with regulations you did not know existed.

*Note: Shadow IT is the enemy of security. If you want to use a new tool, ask first. If I say no, there is a reason.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK WHY YOUR SAAS BILL IS SO HIGH...**

- You signed up for the enterprise plan with 500 users when you have 12 employees. You clicked "Upgrade" without reading the pricing page.
- You added every single feature add-on, premium integration, and advanced analytics package because the buttons were green and inviting.
- You forgot to cancel the free trial. It ended three months ago. The billing has been running every month. The amount is not a mistake. It is the consequence of your enthusiasm.

*Note: Every SaaS free trial is designed to make you forget to cancel. The entire business model depends on it. Do not fall for it.*

---

**BEFORE YOU COMPLAIN THAT SHAREPOINT IS 'TOO COMPLICATED'...**

- SharePoint is a document management system. It has folders, permissions, and version history. It works like a file server but with more steps.
- "I cannot find my file" — you saved it to "General/Documents/Shared/Team/Archive/2024/Q4/Drafts/New Folder (3)/working_copy_final_v2.docx." That is a navigation problem, not a SharePoint problem.
- "SharePoint deleted my file" — SharePoint did not delete it. You dragged it into a subfolder you did not know existed and now you cannot find it. Check the version history. It is still there.

*Note: SharePoint is not complicated. Your filing system is chaotic. Organise your folders before you blame the platform.*

---

**BEFORE YOU DEMAND 'OFFLINE ACCESS' TO YOUR CLOUD FILES...**

- Offline access requires you to sync the files to your local device before you board the flight. You cannot sync files while you are 30,000 feet in the air with no internet.
- "But I need to work on the plane" — then sync the files before you leave for the airport. That is why the "Make Available Offline" button exists.
- If you did not sync it before you lost internet, you do not have it. There is no secret cache in the sky that follows you around. The clue is in the name: "offline" access requires preparation.

*Note: The cloud does not follow you into the sea, the mountains, or the air. Download your files before you leave civilization.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLAIM 'THE CLOUD IS SLOWER THAN THE OFFICE SERVER'...**

- Yes, because the office server is 10 metres away and the cloud server is potentially 1,000 kilometres away. The speed of light is fast but not instantaneous.
- Your 20Mbps internet connection at home is not comparable to the 1Gbps fibre in the office. The cloud is not slow. Your internet is slow.
- Also, the cloud is shared. If everyone in your building is streaming 4K Netflix at lunch, your SaaS application will respond accordingly. That is congestion, not cloud failure.

*Note: The cloud is not magic. It runs on physical servers connected to physical cables. Physics applies.*
