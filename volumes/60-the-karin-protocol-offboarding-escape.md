## The Karin Protocol: The Offboarding Escape

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK TO KEEP YOUR LAPTOP "FOR PERSONAL USE"...**

- "I have had it for years and it feels like mine" — it feels like yours because you have been using it for years. It is not yours. It is a company asset. The company paid for it. The company owns it. You are leaving.
- "I need it to look for a new job" — you need the company laptop to look for a new job while still employed here? That is a different problem. One that HR should probably hear about.
- "I will buy it from you" — you will buy a 4-year-old laptop that is fully depreciated but still contains company data that we cannot guarantee you will wipe. The answer is no. Buy your own laptop.
- "There are personal files on it" — there should not be personal files on it. You were told not to store personal files on it. The personal files are now a data recovery problem. We will return them if we can. You will not keep the laptop.

*Note: The laptop is not a parting gift. It is a company asset that must be returned, wiped, and redeployed or recycled. Your attachment to it is not a policy exception.*

---

**BEFORE YOU FORWARD ALL YOUR COMPANY EMAIL TO YOUR PERSONAL ADDRESS...**

- "I need to keep my emails for my records" — your emails are company records. They belong to the company. Forwarding them to your personal Gmail is a data breach. Do not do it.
- "I have important contacts in there" — the important contacts can be reached via LinkedIn like every other professional on the planet. You do not need 5 years of internal email chains for that.
- "I am just saving a few messages" — you are saving a few messages that contain confidential information. On your personal email. Outside the company's control. That is a policy violation.
- "Nobody will notice" — the system logs every forwarded email. The system has a report. The report goes to compliance. Compliance will notice. I will notice. You will get a very awkward phone call.

*Note: Your company email is not your personal archive. It is not your address book. It is not yours. Leave it behind. That is what "offboarding" means.*

---

**BEFORE YOU WIPE YOUR LAPTOP YOURSELF BEFORE RETURNING IT...**

- "I factory reset it to save you time" — you factory reset it. You also wiped the recovery partition. You also removed the BIOS password. The laptop is now a paperweight that needs to be reimaged from scratch.
- "I deleted my personal files so you do not have to" — you deleted your personal files. You also deleted system files. The laptop now boots to a black screen. The "personal files" were apparently in the Windows directory.
- "I used a program to securely erase everything" — you used a program to securely erase everything. Including the operating system. Including the bootloader. You have created a pile of expensive e-waste.
- "I wanted to make sure my data was safe" — your data was safe. The encrypted drive was safe. The IT wipe process is safe. You did not need to "help." You never need to "help."

*Note: Do not wipe your own laptop. Return it. Let IT do the wipe. The IT wipe process is standard. Your DIY wipe is not.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLAIM YOU "LOST" THE COMPANY DONGLE...**

- "I cannot find it anywhere" — you cannot find it because you lost it. You lost it months ago. You have been using a personal dongle that is not approved. The lost dongle has been missing for longer than your notice period.
- "It might be in my desk" — it is not in your desk. I checked your desk. I found 3 other things that belong to the company. The dongle is not among them.
- "I will keep looking and mail it" — you will not mail it. "I will mail it" is the offboarding equivalent of "the check is in the mail." It never arrives.
- "Can you just write it off?" — I can write it off if you sign a form acknowledging the lost asset and authorizing the replacement cost to be deducted from your final paycheck.

*Note: The dongle is not lost. You lost it. "Lost" is a euphemism for "I was irresponsible and now I do not want to face the consequences." Face them.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ANNOUNCE YOUR DEPARTURE WITH A PASSIVE-AGGRESSIVE GOODBYE EMAIL...**

- "I am leaving for new opportunities" — you are leaving because you were unhappy. Everyone knows you were unhappy. The goodbye email does not need to hint at it. Just say goodbye.
- "I want to thank everyone except..." — you want to thank everyone except specific people. You listed the exceptions. In a company-wide email. That everyone will read. Including the people you excluded.
- "I have learned a lot here" — you have learned a lot here. The next sentence is going to explain why the things you learned are wrong. I can feel it coming. Do not write that sentence.
- "I wish the company well" — "I wish the company well" is corporate for "I hope this place burns down." Everyone reads it that way. Just say "thank you" and stop.

*Note: A goodbye email is not a farewell tour. It is not a performance review. It is not a manifesto. It is a "thank you, here is my contact info, goodbye." Anything else is a liability.*

---

**BEFORE YOU DISABLE YOUR OWN ACCOUNT ON THE WAY OUT...**

- "I changed my password so nobody can access it" — you changed your password to something only you know. Your account now cannot be accessed by IT to transfer your files or forward your email. You have locked us out of your own work.
- "I deleted some files that were personal" — you deleted files that were personal. You also deleted files that were not personal. The audit log shows what you deleted. We have backups. We will restore them. We will know what you deleted.
- "I set up an auto-reply that says 'I do not work here anymore haha'" — the auto-reply is unprofessional. Turn it off. Now.
- "I removed myself from all the distribution lists" — you removed yourself from distribution lists that include active projects. The project teams are now missing emails. Your clean exit just created chaos.

*Note: Do not touch your accounts on the way out. Do not change passwords. Do not delete files. Do not set up auto-replies. Close your laptop. Hand it in. Walk away. That is your only job on the last day.*
