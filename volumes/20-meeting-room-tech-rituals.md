## Meeting Room Tech Rituals

---

**BEFORE YOU BOOK A MEETING ROOM WITHOUT CHECKING THE TECH...**

- The room says "capacity: 10 people." The TV screen is from 2012. The cable is VGA. Your laptop does not have VGA. You have just created a problem that cannot be solved in the 30 seconds before your meeting starts.
- "There was an HDMI cable in the photo" — the photo was taken in 2019. The cable has since been stolen, lost, or chewed by the office dog. Do not trust the room photo.
- A room booked for a video call with 14 participants requires a room that can seat 14 people. That is not the same room you use for a 3-person catch-up.

*Note: Booking a room does not magically equip it with the technology you need. Check the inventory before you book.*

---

**BEFORE YOU START THE MEETING WITHOUT TESTING THE AUDIO...**

- The five minutes of silence at the start of every meeting while someone says "can you hear me?" five times is not mandatory. It is avoidable.
- Testing the microphone before the meeting takes 30 seconds. Those 30 seconds will save 5 minutes of fumbling and 10 minutes of "sorry, can you repeat that?"
- "My audio is not working" — you are muted. You have been muted. The microphone icon has a red line through it. It has been like that since you joined. Everyone has been watching you talk to yourself.

*Note: "Can you hear me?" is the most expensive phrase in corporate audio. Eliminate it from your vocabulary.*

---

**BEFORE YOU STRUGGLE WITH THE HDMI CABLE FOR 10 MINUTES...**

- The HDMI port is on the side of the laptop. Not the USB port. Not the Ethernet port. Not the charging port. The one that looks like a trapezoid with a hole in it.
- If the cable does not fit, do not push harder. Pushing harder will not change the shape of the port. It will break the port.
- There are adapters in the IT drawer. They are labelled. You walked past the IT drawer on the way to the meeting room. Stop pretending you did not see it.

*Note: HDMI cables do not require physical force to insert. If you are struggling, you are doing it wrong. Stop and reconsider your life choices.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BLAME THE MEETING ROOM SYSTEM FOR A BAD MEETING...**

- The Teams Room system is working perfectly. The camera is on. The microphone is receiving audio. The problem is that you are speaking from the far end of the table while looking at your laptop instead of the camera.
- "The screen is not sharing" — you clicked "Share" but did not select which screen or window to share. The system is waiting for you. It will wait forever. It has patience. It is a machine.
- "The call dropped" — the call dropped because someone in this room is on a VPN over Wi-Fi with a weak signal, not because the meeting room system is broken.

*Note: Meeting room tech is not the enemy. It is a neutral party. The enemy is your refusal to learn how to use it.*

---

**BEFORE YOU MOVE THE MEETING ROOM CABLES...**

- The cables are configured in a specific layout to reach the table, connect to the screen, and provide enough slack for the laptop to sit comfortably. You unplugged them and now they do not reach.
- "I just moved it slightly" — you rearranged the entire setup, threaded the cable behind the cabinet, and now it is tangled in the leg of the stand.
- The USB extension cable that was taped to the table was taped there for a reason. You peeled it up. Now the cable falls behind the desk every time someone plugs in.

*Note: If the cables were arranged a specific way, there was a reason. You are not a meeting room ergonomics specialist. Leave the cables alone.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BOOK BACK-TO-BACK MEETINGS IN THE SAME ROOM...**

- The previous meeting ended at 10:00. Your meeting starts at 10:00. The previous meeting ran over by 7 minutes. You are now standing outside the door waiting while 12 people slowly pack up their things.
- The meeting room system needs 2 minutes between sessions to reset the connection and clear the cache. If you do not give it that time, the first 5 minutes of your meeting will be spent staring at someone else's leftovers on the screen.
- "Can you kick the previous meeting out remotely?" — I can, but you will not like the phone call from their manager when the system cuts them off mid-presentation.

*Note: Book a 30-minute slot for a 25-minute meeting. The buffer is not optional. It is survival.*

---

**BEFORE YOU COMPLAIN THAT THE CONFERENCE PHONE IS 'COMPLICATED'...**

- The conference phone has exactly three buttons: Answer, Hang Up, and Volume. You do not need a manual. You need to read the labels on the buttons.
- "It's not connecting" — you pressed the wrong button. You pressed "Hold" instead of "Answer." The other party is on hold. They have been on hold for 4 minutes.
- The phone is not "broken." It is "working exactly as designed but you are pressing the wrong buttons." There is a subtle difference.

*Note: A device with three buttons is not complicated. The problem is not the device.*
