## The Karin Protocol: Tech Shop v1

---

**BEFORE YOU ARGUE THAT THE REPAIR COSTS MORE THAN THE DEVICE IS WORTH...**

- "I could buy a new one on Amazon for $200" — then buy the new one on Amazon for $200. Nobody is stopping you. You came here because you want this one fixed. Pick one.
- "The parts are only $30 on eBay" — the parts are $30 on eBay because they are pulled from a recycled e-waste bin in a country I cannot pronounce. I use new parts. I charge for labor. Labor is not optional.
- "I am not paying $80 to fix a $100 printer" — then do not. Throw it away. That is what "replace" means. "Repair" is the other word. They are different words.
- "For that price I might as well upgrade" — then upgrade. You do not need my permission to spend your money. You need my permission to stop wasting my time.

*Note: The price of a replacement device and the price of a repair are not competing offers. They are two different products. One is a new thing. The other is your old thing, working again. Choose.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TELL ME YOUR NEPHEW "KNOWS COMPUTERS" AND COULD DO IT CHEAPER...**

- "My nephew is really good with this stuff" — your nephew installed RGB fans in his gaming PC. That is not "really good with this stuff." That is adult LEGO.
- "He said it is probably just the RAM" — your nephew diagnosed this without touching it. He diagnosed it from a description you gave him over the phone. He is guessing.
- "He built his own computer" — he assembled parts someone else manufactured, into a case someone else designed, using a tutorial someone else made. He did not build a computer. He followed instructions.
- "I will just have him look at it" — you said that three devices ago. Each time he made it worse. Each time you came back. Each time the repair cost more. Your nephew is my job security.

*Note: Your nephew "knows computers" the same way your cousin "knows cars" because he changes his own oil. It is not the same. It has never been the same.*

---

**BEFORE YOU PULL UP A YOUTUBE VIDEO AND TELL ME HOW TO DO MY JOB...**

- "This guy on YouTube fixed the same issue in 10 minutes" — that video has 12 million views because people watch it instead of paying someone. Most of those people still have broken devices.
- "He used a heat gun and it just popped right off" — he used a heat gun on a device that was already sacrificed for content. That is not a repair. That is a demonstration. My customer's device is not a demonstration.
- "Can you just do what he did?" — I can do what he did. But I will not warranty it, because what he did was wrong. He got views. You get a dead phone.
- "I watched a few tutorials so I kind of know what to expect" — you watched a few tutorials. I have done this 4,000 times. We are not equally qualified.

*Note: YouTube is a video platform. It is not a certification. Watching someone do a thing is not the same as knowing how to do the thing. I watched a documentary about open-heart surgery. You will not let me operate on you.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK ME TO "JUST TAKE A QUICK LOOK" FOR FREE...**

- "Can you just look at it? It will take two seconds" — I have been looking at it for 30 seconds. I already know what is wrong. Knowing what is wrong is the diagnostic. The diagnostic costs money.
- "I just need to know if it is worth fixing" — that is the diagnostic. You are describing the diagnostic. The diagnostic is not free.
- "You do not need to fix it, just tell me what is wrong" — telling you what is wrong is the diagnostic. The diagnostic is a service. Services cost money.
- "I will bring it in if you can give me a quick estimate over the phone" — I cannot diagnose a crackling sound through a phone call. The crackling sound could be a fan, a hard drive, or a tiny man with a hammer. I need to see it.

*Note: Free diagnostics do not exist. What exists is the cost of diagnostics hidden in the price of the repair. You either pay for the diagnostic, or you pay for the diagnostic. Those are the two options.*

---

**BEFORE YOU HAND ME A DEVICE AND SAY "IT WAS WORKING FINE BEFORE I BROUGHT IT IN"...**

- "It was working this morning" — it was working this morning because you did not drop it this morning. You dropped it on the way here. The crack is new. The crack is yours.
- "I did not do anything to it" — you put it in a bag. The bag had a water bottle. The water bottle leaked. The bag is wet. The device is wet. You did something to it.
- "All I did was plug it in" — you plugged in a charger that does not belong to this device. The voltage is wrong. The connector is forced. The port is damaged. "All you did" was enough.
- "I just turned it on and it made a noise" — you turned it on after it fell in the toilet. You did not tell me it fell in the toilet. The noise is the sound of your choices.

*Note: Every device "worked fine" until it did not. The moment it stopped working is the moment you became part of the story. Stop pretending you are just a witness.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING IN A DEVICE COVERED IN FOOD, DUST, OR UNIDENTIFIED SUBSTANCES...**

- "I know it is a little dirty" — it is not a little dirty. There is a ecosystem living in the fan grille. I can identify your lunch from three weeks ago by the fossil record.
- "Can you just clean it while you are in there?" — I can. But "while I am in there" is not free time. It is time. Time costs money.
- "It fell on the floor of my car" — the floor of your car is not a location. It is a biohazard. The device has been in that environment for months. The corrosion is structural now.
- "I do not know what that sticky stuff is" — I do either. I am going to have to treat it as a hazardous material. There will be a surcharge.

*Note: A device covered in the remnants of your lifestyle is a health risk, not a repair job. Clean it before you bring it in, or pay the biohazard fee.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TELL ME WHAT THE OTHER SHOP SAID...**

- "The other shop said it needs a new motherboard" — the other shop said it needs a new motherboard so they could charge you $400 and swap a $15 capacitor. That is why you came to me. Do not quote the liar.
- "I went to three other places and they all gave me different answers" — three different answers means three different guesses. I am about to be the fourth guess. You have not helped anyone by collecting opinions.
- "The guy at [big chain store] said it is not worth fixing" — the guy at the big chain store is paid to sell you a new device. His diagnosis is a sales script. My diagnosis is an actual diagnosis.
- "I do not trust repair shops" — you are in a repair shop. You are asking me to fix something. The trust has already been established by your presence. Do not undermine it in the first sentence.

*Note: If the other shop knew what they were doing, you would not be here. Stop treating their wrong opinion as a valid data point.*
