## The Karin Protocol: The Onboarding Disaster

---

**BEFORE YOU IGNORE THE WELCOME EMAIL AND THEN ASK QUESTIONS THAT WERE ANSWERED IN IT...**

- "I did not see the email" — you did not see the email titled "WELCOME — IMPORTANT — PLEASE READ" with three exclamation marks and a priority flag. You did not see it because you did not read it.
- "There was too much information so I skimmed it" — you skimmed the part that said "your login credentials are below" and missed the credentials. You now need new credentials. You are now a reset request on your first day.
- "Can someone just tell me what to do?" — someone did tell you what to do. The email told you what to do. The email had bullet points. The email had numbered steps. The email was written for someone who cannot be bothered to read it.
- "I will read it later" — later never comes. Later is a myth. Later is the place where unread emails go to die. Read it now. Later is already full.

*Note: The welcome email is not a suggestion. It is a manual for your first week. Read it. Every word. Before you ask a question that is answered in paragraph one.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK FOR A BETTER LAPTOP ON YOUR FIRST DAY...**

- "This one is not what I am used to" — you are used to a different model because your previous company had a different budget. This is not your previous company. This laptop is what we provide. It is sufficient.
- "It is a little slow" — it is a little slow because you installed 14 browser extensions during the 30 minutes since I handed it to you. The laptop is not slow. Your extension addiction is slow.
- "My colleague got a better one" — your colleague has been here for 6 years. Your colleague develops software. Your colleague needs the better one. You are in accounting. You will survive.
- "I need a MacBook" — you need a MacBook because you prefer the aesthetic. The entire company runs on Windows. The software we use is Windows-only. The support we provide is Windows-only. You need a MacBook like a fish needs a bicycle.

*Note: The laptop you receive on your first day is not a reflection of your worth. It is a tool for your role. Use it. If you still need an upgrade after a year of proving yourself, we can talk. Not before.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SET UP YOUR EMAIL SIGNATURE WITH QUOTES, IMAGES, AND RAINBOW COLORS...**

- "I customized it to reflect my personality" — your personality is not a company asset. The email signature is a business communication tool. Keep it professional. Your favorite quote about "living your best life" does not belong in client emails.
- "I added my photo so people know who I am" — people know who you are by your name. The photo takes up half the screen. The photo loads slowly. The photo makes every email you send look like a newsletter.
- "I included a link to my personal blog" — you included a link to your personal blog about your cat. In your company email signature. Every email you send now advertises your cat. Stop it.
- "I made the font bright pink so it stands out" — it stands out. It stands out as unprofessional. The company brand guidelines specify black text. The brand guidelines exist. Read them.

*Note: Your email signature is not a self-expression canvas. It is a corporate communication standard. Name, title, phone number. That is all. The motivational quote stays on your desktop wallpaper.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK FOR ADMIN RIGHTS ON YOUR FIRST WEEK...**

- "I need admin access to install software" — you need admin access to install software that IT has not approved. You do not need that software. You need to ask IT to install approved software. That is the process.
- "I am used to having full control over my machine" — you are used to having full control over your personal machine. This is not your personal machine. This is a company asset. You do not have full control.
- "It will make me more productive" — it will make you more productive at installing software that will break your machine. It will make me less productive at fixing the machine you broke. Net productivity: negative.
- "I know what I am doing" — you know what you are doing on your own computer. You do not know what you are doing on a domain-joined corporate machine with group policy, endpoint protection, and compliance requirements. You know how to click "Yes" on an installer. That is not the same.

*Note: Admin rights are not a status symbol. They are a security risk. You do not need them. If you genuinely do, you will get them after we verify that you actually need them. Not because you asked nicely.*

---

**BEFORE YOU COME TO IT ON YOUR FIRST DAY WITHOUT YOUR LAPTOP CHARGER...**

- "I left it at home" — you left it at home. On your first day. The only day you definitely know you will need it. The day everything is new and the battery is probably dead.
- "Can I borrow one?" — you can borrow one if someone else is willing to work without their charger. Nobody is willing to work without their charger. You are not borrowing a charger.
- "I thought it would last the whole day" — you thought a brand new laptop out of the box would last 8 hours on a partial charge. The battery was at 60% when I gave it to you. It is now 10 AM. The battery is at 20%.
- "Can someone drive me home to get it?" — someone can drive you home to get it if you want to explain to your new manager that you need someone to drive you home to get your charger on your first day.

*Note: Your laptop charger is not optional. It is not a suggestion. It is the only thing keeping your computer alive. Bring it. Every day. Especially the first day.*

---

**BEFORE YOU INTRODUCE YOURSELF TO EVERYONE BY REPLY-ALLING TO THE COMPANY-WIDE ANNOUNCEMENT...**

- "I just wanted to say hello to everyone" — you said hello to 12,000 people. All of them now have a notification. All of them now know your name. All of them now wish you had just said hello to your team.
- "I thought it was the right thing to do" — the right thing to do is reply to your team. The company-wide list is for announcements. You are not an announcement. You are a new hire.
- "I did not realize it would go to everyone" — you did not realize because you did not look at the recipient list. The recipient list said "All Staff (12,000)." You did not check.
- "Can I take it back?" — you cannot take it back. The email is sent. The email is read. The email is archived. Your introduction is now a permanent record of your first mistake.

*Note: The reply-all button is not a greeting card. Do not introduce yourself to the entire company with it. Introduce yourself to your team. Let the rest discover you through your work.*
