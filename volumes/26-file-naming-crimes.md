## File Naming Crimes

---

**BEFORE YOU SAVE THE SAME FILE 17 TIMES WITH DIFFERENT NAMES...**

- Let me guess: `Report.docx`, `Report_v2.docx`, `Report_v3.docx`, `Report_FINAL.docx`, `Report_FINAL_REAL.docx`, `Report_FINAL_REAL_2.docx`, `Report_FINAL_REAL_3_USE_THIS.docx`, `Report_FINAL_REAL_3_USE_THIS_OMG.docx`, `Report_ACTUALLY_FINAL.docx`
- Every single one of these is a different version of the same document, all in the same folder, with no indication of what changed between them.
- Version control software exists. Git exists. Even "Save As" with a date stamp would be an improvement over whatever this chaos is.

*Note: A folder with 47 files all named "final" with slight variations is not a filing system. It is a cry for help.*

---

**BEFORE YOU NAME A FILE 'Document1.docx'...**

- You saved a new document, and instead of giving it a descriptive name, you pressed Enter on the default "Document1" and closed it forever.
- In three weeks, you will need that file. You will search for it. "Document1" will not help you. There are 14 files named "Document1" on your drive.
- The 10 seconds it takes to type a meaningful filename now will save you 30 minutes of frantic searching later. This is not complicated.

*Note: "Document1" is not a filename. It is a placeholder that you accepted as permanent. Name your files like an adult.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE ONLY DATES TO NAME FILES...**

- `2024-01-15.docx`, `2024-02-03.docx`, `2024-03-22.docx` — great, you know when you created them. But what ARE they? Meeting minutes? A report? A grocery list?
- Dates are useful AS PART of a filename, not as the ENTIRE filename. Combine the date with a descriptive title. This is not an either/or situation.
- "I will know what it is by the date" — you will not. Future you will curse past you for this arrogance.

*Note: A filename that only contains a date is a timestamp, not a label. You are not an archive system.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SAVE FILES TO THE DESKTOP 'FOR EASY ACCESS'...**

- Your desktop has 147 files on it. They fill the entire screen. The wallpaper is completely obscured. You cannot see the recycle bin. This is not "easy access." This is digital hoarding.
- "I know where everything is" — you do not. You scroll for 30 seconds every time you need a file. You squint at tiny icons and read truncated filenames. This is not efficiency. It is denial.
- The desktop is not a filing cabinet. It is a temporary workspace. Files belong in folders. Folders belong in directories. Directories belong on drives. This hierarchy exists for a reason.

*Note: A desktop cluttered with files is not a badge of productivity. It is a symptom of disorganisation.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE ALL CAPS IN FILENAMES...**

- `REPORT_FINAL_REALLY_IMPORTANT_DO_NOT_DELETE_V2.DOCX` — I can see it is important. I can also see you have no restraint.
- All caps in filenames is the digital equivalent of shouting. The file is not shouting. You are shouting on behalf of the file.
- Also, YOU_CANNOT_PUT_SPACES_IN_FILENAMES_LIKE_THIS_EITHER. Underscores are not spaces. They are underscores. They do not make the filename better.

*Note: A filename should describe the content, not your emotional state about the content. Lowercase is allowed. Use it.*

---

**BEFORE YOU STORE CRITICAL FILES ONLY IN THE RECYCLE BIN...**

- The recycle bin is not a storage location. It is a holding cell for files awaiting deletion. Files in the recycle bin are one "Empty Recycle Bin" click away from oblivion.
- "I keep my important files there so I can restore them if I delete them by accident" — that is not how any of this works. That is like parking your car in a crusher "just in case."
- The recycle bin is emptied automatically on a schedule. Your "important" files are being routinely purged by a process that does not know they are special.

*Note: Storing files in the recycle bin is not a backup strategy. It is a ticking time bomb. Move your files to a real folder.*
