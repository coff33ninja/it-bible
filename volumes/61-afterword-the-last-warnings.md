## Afterword — The Last Warnings You Will Ever Need

---

**BEFORE YOU DO ANYTHING, READ THE THING...**

- The instructions were on the screen. You clicked "Next" without reading them. The default option was "Delete everything." You now have a screenshot of a blank screen and a support ticket asking "What happened?" Reading takes 10 seconds. Recovery takes 2 days.
- The email said "Your account will be deactivated if you do not respond by Friday." You responded on Monday. Your account is deactivated. The deadline was not a suggestion. It was a condition. You failed the condition.
- The pop-up said "This action cannot be undone." You clicked OK. The action cannot be undone. You are now asking IT to undo the action that the system explicitly told you cannot be undone. The system was not lying. It was warning you. You ignored it.
- The error message contained the solution. It said "Insufficient permissions. Contact your administrator." You contacted your administrator and said "It does not work." You did not include the error message. You made the administrator ask for it. The answer was in the message you did not share.

*Note: The text on the screen is not decoration. It is documentation. Read it before you click, and you will not need to ask what happened.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BLAME THE COMPUTER...**

- You said "The computer is slow today." You have 47 browser tabs open, a video call running, an antivirus scan in progress, and Spotify streaming. The computer is not slow. The computer is doing 47 things because you asked it to do 47 things.
- You said "The internet is down." Your Wi-Fi is disconnected. You clicked the Wi-Fi icon and saw a list of networks. Your network was at the top. You did not click it. You closed the list and called IT. The internet was not down. Your willingness to click one more button was down.
- You said "The program crashed for no reason." The event log shows an error. The error has a code. The code has a description. The description says "Out of memory." The program crashed because you had 47 tabs open. The reason was on your screen. You did not look.
- You said "Nothing changed, it just stopped working." Windows Update installed a restart. The restart was pending for 3 days. You clicked "Remind me later" 12 times. The 13th time, Windows reminded you by restarting. The change happened. You deferred it until it was forced.

*Note: The computer is a mirror. It reflects what you told it to do. If you do not like what it is doing, check what you told it to do. The problem is usually between the chair and the keyboard.*

**BEFORE YOU SAY 'I DID NOT KNOW'...**

- You did not know the backup policy existed. It was sent to you in onboarding. It was posted in the company wiki. It was mentioned in the team meeting. You did not read it, you did not check the wiki, and you did not listen in the meeting. "I did not know" is not a defence. It is a confession of inattention.
- You did not know that clicking the link would install malware. The link was from an unknown sender. The subject line was "Invoice." You do not handle invoices. You clicked it anyway. The email filter warned you. The browser warned you. The antivirus warned you. You clicked through all three warnings.
- You did not know the file was too large to email. The attachment was 200 MB. The email client said "File exceeds size limit." You clicked "Send" anyway. The email sat in your outbox for 3 hours before failing. You did not check. You assumed it went through. The client did not get the file. The deadline was missed.
- You did not know the password expired every 90 days. The notification said "Your password expires in 7 days." You dismissed it. The notification said "Your password expires tomorrow." You dismissed it. The notification said "Your password has expired." You called IT. The warning was given. You dismissed it three times.

*Note: "I did not know" is not an excuse the first time. By the third time, it is a pattern. IT sends warnings, notifications, and policies. Your ignorance is not an accident. It is a choice to ignore.*

**BEFORE YOU ASK IT FOR HELP WITHOUT TRYING ANYTHING...**

- "My email is not working" — did you check your internet connection? Did you restart Outlook? Did you check if the server is down? Did you try webmail? No. You opened a ticket. The answer was "restart Outlook." The ticket took longer than the fix.
- "My computer will not turn on" — is it plugged in? Is the power cable connected? Is the battery charged? Did you try a different outlet? No. You called IT. The cable was loose. The fix was pushing a cable into a socket. You called IT to push a cable.
- "I cannot print" — is the printer on? Does it have paper? Does it have ink? Is it connected to the network? Did you select the correct printer? No. You submitted a ticket. The printer was off. You turned it off yesterday and forgot. IT turned it on.
- "The website is down" — is it down for everyone or just you? Did you check on your phone? Did you clear your cache? Did you try a different browser? No. You reported a site-wide outage. The site was up. Your browser cache was corrupted. Clearing it fixed it.

*Note: Before you contact IT, ask yourself: have I tried anything? If the answer is no, try something. Restart. Check cables. Check settings. The problem that fixes itself is the problem that did not need a ticket.*

**BEFORE YOU FORGET THAT IT PEOPLE ARE PEOPLE...**

- You yelled at the IT support person because your email was down for 10 minutes. The support person was handling 40 other tickets. Your email came back after 12 minutes. The outage was not their fault. The yelling was your choice. They remember. They will remember when your next issue is urgent.
- You said "This is urgent" about a problem that has existed for 3 weeks. Urgency is not a property of time. It is a property of impact. A problem that has waited 3 weeks is not urgent. It is neglected. The actual urgent issues are now delayed because you cried wolf.
- You CC'd the CEO on a support ticket because the response time was 4 hours instead of 2. The ticket was for a font not displaying correctly. The CEO now has a ticket about fonts in their inbox. The support person now has a meeting with their manager about the escalation. The font is still not displaying correctly. The escalation did not fix it.
- You said "You people never fix anything" to the person who fixed your computer last week, the month before, and the month before that. The person who keeps fixing your computer is the same person you are insulting. They fix your things repeatedly. You insult them repeatedly. One of these patterns is sustainable.

*Note: IT support is not a vending machine. It is a relationship. The people who fix your problems are people. Treat them like people, and your problems will be fixed faster. Treat them like machines, and you become the problem.*

**BEFORE YOU CONTINUE EXISTING WITHOUT BASIC READING COMPREHENSION...**

- You had ONE JOB. The dialog box said "DO YOU WANT TO INSTALL THIS SOFTWARE?" There was a picture of the software. There was the publisher name. There was a CANCEL button. You clicked YES. You do not KNOW what you installed. You do not KNOW who published it. You did not READ the three words on the button you clicked. The toolbar is now on your browser. The ads are now in your face. The malware is now on your network. All because reading THREE WORDS was too much effort for your two functioning brain cells.
- You saw an ad that said "YOUR PC HAS A VIRUS! CLICK HERE TO SCAN!" The ad was flashing. The ad had Comic Sans. The ad was on a website called "totallyfreegames.ru." The ad was next to an ad for "ONE WEIRD TRICK TO LOSE BELLY FAT." You clicked it. You downloaded the "scanner." You ran the "scanner." The "scanner" was the virus. The virus is now encrypting your files. The ransom note is written in the same broken English as the ad. You paid the scam, you downloaded the scam, you ran the scam. You completed the scam's entire to-do list for them. You did the scam's job better than the scammer did.
- The installer had 7 screens. Each screen had checkboxes. The checkboxes said things like "Install browser toolbar," "Change default search engine to Bing," "Make CNBC your homepage," "Install coupon finder extension." You clicked "Next >>" 7 times without reading a single word. Your browser is now a carnival of unwanted toolbars. Your homepage is a search engine that does not know what a search engine is. Your new tab page is covered in coupons for things you would never buy. You did this to yourself. The installer asked permission at every step. You granted it. You are not a victim. You are a volunteer.
- You tried to install a PDF reader. You searched "pdf reader free download." You clicked the first result. It was not the PDF reader. It was a "download manager" that bundles adware. The real PDF reader was the fourth result. You did not scroll. You did not check the URL. You clicked the first link like a toddler grabbing the brightest candy on the shelf. The candy is malware. The shelf is the internet. You are not old enough to be left unsupervised.
- The pop-up asked for your email address to "claim your prize." You do not remember entering a contest. The pop-up does not say which contest. The pop-up does not say what the prize is. The pop-up is a pop-up. It is asking for your email. You gave it. Your inbox is now a landfill of pharmaceutical spam. The prize was more spam. You won. Congratulations.
- You clicked "UNSUBSCRIBE" on a phishing email. The email was from "totallynotascam@phish.ru." The email said your Netflix account was suspended. You do not have Netflix. You clicked UNSUBSCRIBE anyway. You confirmed your email is active. You confirmed you click links in phishing emails. You are now on every spam list on the dark web. Your email address is worth money because of you. YOU are the product. YOU are the supply chain. YOU are the reason spam exists.
- The warning said "THIS FILE MAY BE DANGEROUS." It said "ARE YOU SURE YOU WANT TO RUN THIS?" It said "THIS FILE IS FROM AN UNKNOWN PUBLISHER." It said "RUNNING THIS MAY HARM YOUR COMPUTER." There were 4 warnings. You bypassed all 4. You clicked "More info." You clicked "Run anyway." You typed your administrator password. You did all of this to open a file called "document.exe" that was attached to an email from "shipping@freemailservice.net" about a package you did not order. The document was not a document. The exe was not a document. The shipping company does not send executables. The four warnings were not Microsoft being annoying. They were Microsoft trying harder to protect you from yourself than you are willing to protect yourself.

*Note: Reading is not a superpower. It is a basic human function that you have decided to opt out of. Every dialog box, every warning, every confirmation screen exists because someone before you ignored it and broke something. The text is not there to slow you down. It is there to save you. But you will not read this note either. You have already scrolled past it. Congratulations. You proved the point.*
