## The Karin Protocol: The Security Risk

---

**BEFORE YOU CLICK THE LINK IN THE EMAIL THAT SAYS "YOUR ACCOUNT HAS BEEN COMPROMISED"...**

- "It looked legit" — it looked legit because it had a logo on it. Anyone can put a logo on an email. The Nigerian prince also had a logo. The link goes to "secure-login.companyname-verify.com." That is not our domain.
- "I was just trying to see what would happen" — what happened is you entered your credentials on a phishing site. What happened is your account is now compromised. What happened is what was always going to happen.
- "It asked for my password and I realized it might be fake" — you realized it might be fake after you typed your password. Before you pressed enter. The password was already sent. The realization was too late.
- "I get so many of these emails, how am I supposed to know?" — you are supposed to know by checking the sender address, hovering over the link, and using the common sense that god gave a turnip. The training exists. Take it.

*Note: If an email asks for your password, it is a scam. Full stop. No legitimate service asks for your password by email. No exception. No "but this one looked real." None.*

---

**BEFORE YOU PLUG IN A USB DRIVE YOU FOUND IN THE PARKING LOT...**

- "I found it in the parking lot and wanted to see whose it was" — you wanted to see whose it was by plugging it into your work computer. Instead of checking with IT. Instead of doing literally anything else.
- "It has a label that says 'Q4 Bonuses'" — the label says "Q4 Bonuses" because the attacker wants you to plug it in. The label is bait. You are the fish. The hook is malware.
- "I thought it might be important" — it is important. It is important to the person who wants to infect your computer. That person left it there. For you. To find. You found it.
- "I scanned it with antivirus first" — you scanned a USB drive found in a parking lot. The antivirus did not detect anything because the malware is zero-day. Your scan gave you false confidence. The USB drive is still dangerous.

*Note: USB drives found in parking lots are not lost property. They are attack vectors. Treat every unknown USB drive as if it is coated in anthrax. Because digitally, it is.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK WHY YOU CANNOT INSTALL "FREE" SOFTWARE FROM THE INTERNET...**

- "It is free, so it cannot be a virus" — "free" and "safe" are not the same thing. "Free" is the price. "Virus" is the product. You are the product.
- "I just need this one tool for a project" — the tool is "PDFConverterPro.exe." It converts PDFs into 47 toolbars, a new default search engine, and a cryptocurrency miner. The tool is a delivery mechanism.
- "My friend recommended it" — your friend recommended it because your friend already installed it and now their computer is also compromised. Your friend is not a malware researcher. Your friend is a vector.
- "I downloaded it from the official website" — the "official website" is the first Google result. The first Google result is a sponsored ad. The sponsored ad redirects to a domain that is three days old. That is not official.

*Note: If you did not get the software from your IT department or the official app store, you got it from somewhere that wants to compromise your machine. Free software is not free. It has a price. The price is your security.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TAKE A PICTURE OF YOUR SCREEN THAT CONTAINS CONFIDENTIAL DATA...**

- "I needed to show my friend something" — your friend does not have clearance for this data. The photo is now on your personal phone. The photo is backed up to your personal cloud. The photo is now a data breach.
- "I cropped out the sensitive part" — you cropped out the column header. The data is still visible. I can still read customer names. Your crop job is not redaction.
- "I will delete it after" — you will not delete it after. You will forget about it. The photo will live in your camera roll forever. Next to photos of your cat.
- "It was just easier than explaining it" — easier for you. Harder for the compliance team. Harder for the legal team. Harder for everyone who has to clean up your convenience-driven data leak.

*Note: Taking photos of confidential data with your personal phone is a breach. Every time. No exceptions. Use the approved tools. The approved tools exist for this exact reason.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CONNECT YOUR WORK LAPTOP TO PUBLIC WI-FI WITHOUT THE VPN...**

- "The Starbucks Wi-Fi is password protected" — the Starbucks Wi-Fi password is printed on a receipt. Everyone who buys a coffee gets the password. The password is not security. The password is a formality.
- "I forgot to turn on the VPN" — you forgot to turn on the VPN. Your traffic is now visible to everyone on the network. Your emails are visible. Your documents are visible. Your passwords are visible.
- "I will just quickly check my email" — you will quickly check your email over unencrypted public Wi-Fi. "Quickly" is all it takes. A packet capture does not take long. A credential harvest does not take long.
- "The VPN makes everything slow" — the VPN makes everything slow because it is encrypting your traffic. That encryption is the only thing protecting you from the teenager in the corner with a laptop and a Python script.

*Note: Public Wi-Fi is not a network. It is a public square where everyone can see your traffic. The VPN is not optional. The VPN is the only thing between you and the person monitoring the network. Turn it on.*

---

**BEFORE YOU TURN OFF YOUR ANTIVIRUS BECAUSE "IT IS SLOWING DOWN YOUR COMPUTER"...**

- "I disabled it temporarily and forgot to turn it back on" — you disabled it temporarily. That was three months ago. The antivirus has not been running for three months. Your computer has been unprotected for three months.
- "It keeps blocking things I want to download" — it keeps blocking things you want to download because the things you want to download are malicious. The antivirus is doing its job. You are doing the opposite of your job.
- "I have not had any issues since I turned it off" — you have not had any issues that you can see. The malware running silently in the background is not going to announce itself. It is going to wait.
- "I know what I am doing" — you know what you are doing when you disable the security software that the company installed specifically to protect against people who do not know what they are doing.

*Note: Disabling antivirus is not a performance optimization. It is a security nullification. The antivirus is not the problem. Your impatience is the problem.*
