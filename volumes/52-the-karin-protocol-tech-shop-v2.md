## The Karin Protocol: Tech Shop v2

---

**BEFORE YOU HAND ME A DEVICE WITH A LOCKED SCREEN AND NO PASSWORD...**

- "I forgot the password" — you forgot the password to the device you use every day. You use it every day. You forgot. I cannot guess it. I am not a psychic. I am a technician.
- "It is my dead husband's phone and I need the photos" — I am sorry for your loss. I still cannot bypass the lock screen without proof of ownership. Grief is not authentication.
- "Just wipe it and start over" — you want me to wipe the device without being able to verify you own it. That is not a repair request. That is a felony I am not going to help you commit.
- "Can you crack the password?" — I can crack the password if you bring me a court order, a police report, and a notarized letter. I cannot crack the password because you locked yourself out of your own iPad for the fourth time.

*Note: A device with a locked screen is a brick until you prove it is yours. I am not a locksmith. I am not a hacker. I am the person who needs you to type the password before I can help you.*

---

**BEFORE YOU DEMAND YOUR REPAIR BE DONE BEFORE EVERYONE ELSE'S...**

- "I need it by 5 PM" — it is 4:30 PM. The diagnostic alone takes longer than 30 minutes. You need a time machine, not a technician.
- "I am leaving on a flight tomorrow" — you have known about this flight for months. You broke your laptop today. Your poor planning is not my emergency.
- "Can you just prioritize mine?" — everyone who walks through that door is a priority to them. You are not special. Your urgency is not a unique burden.
- "I will pay extra to skip the line" — you will pay extra. Everyone who skips the line pays extra. You are not the first person to discover that money talks. The line is still a line.

*Note: The word "rush" does not mean "do it now." It means "do it faster than usual." The word "ASAP" does not mean "right this second." It means "at some point sooner than if you had not asked." Learn the definitions.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING IN A DEVICE YOU ALREADY "FIXED" AND MADE WORSE...**

- "I tried to replace the screen myself" — you tried to replace the screen yourself and now the frame is bent, the ribbon cable is torn, and there are three screws left over that you "could not find a home for." The screws were important.
- "I watched a guide and got halfway through before I got stuck" — you got stuck because the guide said "remove the adhesive carefully" and you ripped the digitizer off with pliers. You did not get stuck. You deviated.
- "I took it apart to clean it and now it won't turn on" — you took it apart with a butter knife and a flathead screwdriver that belongs in a toolbox from 1987. You did not clean it. You committed a hate crime against electronics.
- "I replaced the battery but now the screen does not work" — you replaced the battery and forgot to disconnect the power before unplugging the display. The backlight is fried. The screen was collateral damage in your adventure.

*Note: If you open a device without the right tools, the right training, or the right brain, you are not "attempting a repair." You are "creating a more expensive problem for a professional to fix." Congratulations.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK ME TO INSTALL PARTS YOU BOUGHT ONLINE...**

- "I bought the part on Amazon, can you just put it in?" — you bought a part from a seller named "Electronics_Deals_2025" with 12 reviews and a name that looks like it was generated by a bot. I am not installing that. It is not a part. It is a gamble.
- "It said 'compatible with your model' in the title" — the title also said "100% original" and the listing has a photo of a part that is clearly a different color. The title is not a specification. The title is a trap.
- "If it does not work, I will just buy another one" — you will just buy another one and then come back and ask me to install that one too. I am not a test bench for your eBay addiction.
- "The seller said it is genuine" — the seller said it is genuine. The seller is a person called "tech_parts_4u" operating out of a warehouse that does not exist. The part is not genuine. The part is a lie.

*Note: Bringing in your own parts does not make the repair cheaper. It makes the repair riskier, the warranty void, and my hourly rate the same. You saved money on the part. You will spend it on labor when the part fails.*

---

**BEFORE YOU REFUSE TO PAY THE DIAGNOSTIC FEE...**

- "I am not paying $50 just for you to tell me it is broken" — I just told you it is broken. It took me an hour to confirm what kind of broken. That hour is the diagnostic. The $50 is for the hour.
- "You did not even fix anything" — I identified the problem. That is the first half of fixing it. You cannot skip the first half and call it overpriced.
- "I could have figured that out myself" — you could have. You did not. That is why you are here. The diagnostic fee is the penalty for not figuring it out yourself.
- "Just waive the fee and I will come back for the repair" — you will not come back. I have seen this dance before. You will take the free diagnosis to another shop or try to fix it yourself and make it worse. The diagnostic fee is non-refundable because the diagnostic is a service.

*Note: The diagnostic fee is not optional. It is not negotiable. It is not a suggestion. It is the price of my expertise. You are paying for the years of experience that let me identify your problem in an hour instead of a week. That is not expensive. That is a bargain.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING IN A DEVICE THAT HAS BEEN SITTING IN A DRAWER FOR TWO YEARS AND EXPECT IT TO WORK...**

- "It was fine when I put it away" — it was fine when you put it away in 2023. The battery has discharged to zero. The software has not been updated in three major versions. "Fine" was a temporary condition.
- "I just need to get the photos off" — the photos are on a storage chip that is powered by a battery that is now a spicy pillow. I can get the photos off. It will involve microsoldering. It will not be cheap.
- "I charged it for an hour and it still won't turn on" — you charged a battery that has been in deep discharge for 18 months. The battery is chemically dead. Charging it for an hour is like putting a drop of water in a desert and expecting a lake.
- "It was working before I stored it" — storage is not a pause button. Devices degrade. Batteries decay. Connectors corrode. Time passes. Your device aged while it sat in that drawer. It is not the same device you put away.

*Note: A device is not a frozen meal. You cannot leave it in a drawer for two years and expect it to work like the day you bought it. Electronics have an expiration date. That date does not pause when the device is off.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK ME TO "RECOVER THE DATA" FROM A DEVICE THAT WAS RUN OVER BY A CAR...**

- "It just needs a new screen" — the device is bent into a U-shape. The screen is not the problem. The laws of physics are the problem. You need a miracle, not a repair.
- "The data is probably still there" — the data is probably still on the NAND chip. The NAND chip is currently curved. Curved NAND chips do not always cooperate.
- "My whole life is on this phone" — your whole life was on this phone. Now your whole life is in a plastic bag that I am holding with tweezers. We will try our best.
- "How much would it cost to get everything back?" — it would cost between "a lot" and "more than the phone is worth" depending on how badly you want those photos of your cat from 2019.

*Note: Data recovery from a physically destroyed device is not a repair. It is a rescue mission. The outcome is not guaranteed. The price reflects the uncertainty. And no, we do not have a "fix it for free if you fail" option.*
