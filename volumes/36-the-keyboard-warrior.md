## The Keyboard Warrior

---

**BEFORE YOU HAMMER THE KEYBOARD WHEN IT IS SLOW...**

- Hitting the keyboard harder does not make the computer process faster. The keys are not pressure-sensitive accelerators. The computer does not respond to percussive maintenance.
- You have cracked the keycap of the Enter key. You have worn the letter 'E' off the keyboard completely. The space bar is sticking because you have compressed the spring with years of aggressive thumb strikes.
- "The computer was lagging so I hit it" — the computer was lagging because of a network timeout. Hitting it did not resolve the timeout. It just broke the keyboard. Now you need a new keyboard AND a network fix.

*Note: A keyboard is an input device, not a punching bag. Aggressive typing does not speed up processing. It just means you type aggressively.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BRING ME A KEYBOARD WITH MISSING KEYCAPS...**

- You have been using this keyboard for 3 years. The 'A' key is missing. The 'S' key is missing. The 'Ctrl' key is held on with hope. You have adapted to typing around the gaps. This is not "making do." This is suffering in silence.
- "I did not want to bother you" — you are bothering me now, with a keyboard that looks like it survived a war. This could have been a 30-second replacement 2 years ago.
- The keycaps did not disappear naturally. You pried one off to clean under it, lost it, and never reported it. You have been typing 'A' by stabbing at the exposed switch mechanism.

*Note: A keyboard with missing keys is not a "bit of character." It is neglect. Keyboards cost R150. Replace them before they look like dental models of a meth addict.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SPILL LIQUID ON YOUR KEYBOARD AND SAY NOTHING...**

- You spilled coffee on your keyboard. It was working "mostly fine" afterwards, so you did not report it. The sticky residue has now corroded the membrane layer, causing intermittent key registration and random character input.
- "It was just a little" — it was enough. The sugar in the coffee has crystallised between the layers. The keys are sticking. The keyboard is typing 'kkkkkkkk' every time you press 'k.'
- You hid a liquid-damaged keyboard and let it slowly die over 3 months instead of reporting it immediately when it could have been cleaned. Now it is a biohazard.

*Note: A keyboard that smells like last week's coffee and types like it is having a seizure is not "still working." It is a health hazard and a productivity disaster.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE A LOUD MECHANICAL KEYBOARD IN AN OPEN-PLAN OFFICE...**

- Your custom mechanical keyboard with Cherry MX Blue switches clicks audibly with every keystroke. Every single keystroke. All 80 words per minute of them. The entire office can hear your typing. They hate it.
- "I need the tactile feedback" — what you need is an awareness that you work in a shared space. The person sitting 3 metres from you can hear every click. They have considered violence.
- Open-plan offices are not LAN gaming cafes. Your clack-clack-clack is not impressive. It is hostile. Buy a quiet keyboard or work from home.

*Note: A loud mechanical keyboard in an open-plan office is not a productivity tool. It is a declaration of war against your colleagues.*

---

**BEFORE YOU CLAIM THE KEYBOARD 'JUST STOPPED WORKING'...**

- The keyboard stopped working because the USB cable has been stretched taught across your desk for 18 months, slowly stressing the internal wires until one finally snapped. This was not sudden. This was gradual and predictable.
- You have been eating crisps over this keyboard. There are crumbs under the space bar, between the keys, and inside the switch housings. The crumbs have attracted moisture, which has caused corrosion.
- "I did not do anything to it" — you did not maintain it. You did not clean it. You did not treat it with care. It failed because of accumulated neglect, not spontaneous mechanical failure.

*Note: Keyboards do not "just stop working." They fail because of how they were treated. Every crumb, every stretch, every aggressive keystroke contributed to this moment.*
