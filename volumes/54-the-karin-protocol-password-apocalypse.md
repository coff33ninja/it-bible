## The Karin Protocol: The Password Apocalypse

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK IT TO RESET YOUR PASSWORD FOR THE 47TH TIME THIS MONTH...**

- "I forgot my password again" — you did not forget your password. You entered the wrong one three times and the system locked you out. The password is the same one we reset yesterday.
- "It says my password is expired" — your password is set to expire every 90 days. It has been 91 days. The system did exactly what it was configured to do. You are not a victim. You are late.
- "Can you just tell me what it is?" — I cannot tell you what it is because I do not know what it is. Passwords are hashed. They are not stored in a spreadsheet called "passwords.txt" on my desktop.
- "I have too many passwords to remember" — you have too many passwords because you reset them every time you forget instead of writing them down in a password manager. The password manager is the solution you refuse to adopt.

*Note: Your password is not broken. Your memory is not broken. Your process is broken. Use a password manager. It is 2026. This is not new information.*

---

**BEFORE YOU WRITE YOUR PASSWORD ON A STICKY NOTE AND ATTACH IT TO YOUR MONITOR...**

- "I know it is not secure but I have to write it down" — you have to write it down because you refuse to use a password manager. The sticky note is not the solution. It is the symptom.
- "Nobody can see it from here" — I can see it from here. I am standing at your desk. I can read it from across the room. It is "Password1!" You have used "Password1!" since 2018.
- "I work from home so it is fine" — you work from home until you do not. The sticky note will stay on the monitor when you go to the office. The sticky note does not know you changed locations.
- "I will remember to take it down before visitors come" — you will not. You never do. There are sticky notes on your monitor from 2022. They have passwords for systems that no longer exist.

*Note: A sticky note with a password is not a memory aid. It is a security breach with adhesive backing.*

---

**BEFORE YOU COMPLAIN THAT TWO-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION IS "TOO HARD"...**

- "It is so inconvenient to get my phone every time" — it takes 12 seconds. You spend more time sighing about it than doing it. The 12 seconds are worth not having your email hijacked by a ransomware gang.
- "Can you just disable 2FA for my account?" — I can disable 2FA for your account. I can also leave your front door unlocked with a sign that says "nobody home." Both are equally bad ideas.
- "I keep losing my authentication codes" — you keep losing them because you delete the backup codes instead of saving them. The backup codes are for backup. Save them.
- "The code expired before I could type it" — the code expires in 30 seconds. You stared at your phone for 25 seconds before starting to type. The code did not expire too fast. You were too slow.

*Note: Two-factor authentication is not optional. It is not negotiable. It is not "too hard." It is the bare minimum for security in a world where your password is probably "Password1!"*

---

**BEFORE YOU SHARE YOUR PASSWORD WITH A COWORKER...**

- "I gave Bob my password so he could finish the report" — Bob now has access to everything you have access to. Bob should have his own access. Bob does not need your password. Bob needs a ticket.
- "We share an account in our department" — you share an account because you refused to set up shared mailboxes. The audit trail is now useless. I cannot tell who did what. I only know that "Karin" did everything.
- "I trust them, they are my friend" — your friend is a person who changes jobs. Your friend is a person who gets disgruntled. Your friend is a person who could make a "mistake" that gets traced back to you. Trust is not a security control.
- "It is easier than setting up a new account" — it is easier for you. It is harder for me. It is impossible for the security team. It is a disaster waiting to happen.

*Note: Sharing passwords is not teamwork. It is a shared liability. When something goes wrong, there is no way to prove who did it. And something will go wrong.*

---

**BEFORE YOU RESET YOUR PASSWORD TO THE SAME THING WITH A NUMBER AT THE END...**

- "I changed it to Welcome2!" — you changed it to Welcome2! because Welcome1! expired. Next month it will be Welcome3! The pattern is not clever. It is predictable.
- "The system said it cannot be the same as the last 10 passwords" — the system is configured to prevent exactly what you are trying to do. The system has seen every variation of your cat's name.
- "I just rotated the characters" — you took "Summer2024!" and turned it into "!4202remmuS". You think this is a new password. It is the same password with a haircut.
- "I used the password generator but I already forgot what it generated" — the password generator gave you something secure. You immediately forgot it. You reset it to something you can remember. Something like "Password1!"

*Note: Incrementing a number at the end of your password is not a password change. It is a ritual that security systems have been trained to detect since 2012.*

---

**BEFORE YOU DEMAND TO KNOW WHY YOU NEED A PASSWORD AT ALL...**

- "Why can we not just use fingerprints?" — we use fingerprints. You registered your fingerprint. You also need a password because fingerprints can be copied and you cannot change your fingers.
- "Nobody would want to hack MY account" — nobody wants to hack your account. They want to use your account to send spam, launch attacks, and access systems that are not yours. You are not the target. You are the stepping stone.
- "This is a waste of time" — resetting your password every week because you refuse to use a password manager is a waste of time. The password policy is not the problem. You are the problem.
- "Just let me in, I am in a hurry" — you are always in a hurry. You are always locked out. The two conditions are connected. Rushing causes mistakes. Mistakes cause lockouts. Lockouts cause rushing. Break the cycle.

*Note: You need a password because the alternative is a world where anyone can access anything. That world is called "before security existed." We tried it. It was terrible.*
