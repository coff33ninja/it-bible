## The Over-Explainer

---

**BEFORE YOU START YOUR TICKET WITH YOUR LIFE STORY...**

- "So I was born in 1987..." — your birth year is not relevant to why Excel is not responding. Skip to the part where things broke.
- "Let me give you some background" — the background is that something is broken. The foreground is that I need to fix it. Do not bury the lede.
- "I am not very technical, so bear with me" — I have been bearing with you since paragraph one. You are on paragraph seven.
- "This all started when we migrated from the old system three years ago" — no. This started 10 minutes ago when you clicked something you should not have clicked.

*Note: A ticket is not a memoir. A ticket is a problem statement. Write it like one.*

---

**BEFORE YOU EXPLAIN THE PROBLEM USING ANALOGIES...**

- "It is like when you try to put a square peg in a round hole" — it is not like that. Tell me what the actual error message says.
- "Imagine you are driving a car and the engine makes a noise" — I am not driving a car. I am reading a ticket. What does the computer do?
- "You know how when you pour water into a full glass it overflows?" — I know how fluid dynamics work. I do not know why your printer is offline.

*Note: Every minute you spend crafting an analogy is a minute you could have spent copying the error message.*

---

**BEFORE YOU INCLUDE EVERY ATTEMPT YOU MADE BEFORE CONTACTING IT...**

- "I tried restarting, then I tried unplugging it, then I tried yelling at it" — you tried yelling at it. That is not a troubleshooting step. That is a ceremony.
- "I Googled it and tried three things from a forum" — you do not know what those three things did. You may have made it worse.
- "I reinstalled the software before calling you" — you reinstalled the software. That is destruction of evidence. Do not tamper with the crime scene.

*Note: If you attempted a fix before contacting IT, you are now a suspect, not a witness.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SEND A SCREENSHOT WITH CIRCLE ANNOTATIONS, ARROWS, AND REDACTIONS...**

- "I circled the thing" — you circled the clock. The error message is in the bottom right. You circled the system tray.
- "I put an arrow pointing to where it says error" — thank you. I would not have known what "error" meant without the arrow.
- "I blacked out my personal information" — you blacked out the error message too. Now I cannot read either.

*Note: The more effort you put into annotating a screenshot, the less useful the screenshot becomes. This is a law of nature.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SUMMARISE THE PROBLEM IN THE LAST SENTENCE OF A FOUR-PARAGRAPH EMAIL...**

- "Anyway, long story short: my email is not working" — that is the problem. That is the sentence that should have been the subject line.
- "Sorry for the long email, I ramble when I am frustrated" — your frustration is valid. Your rambling is not.
- "TL;DR: I cannot print" — you could have led with that. The first 300 words were about your morning commute.

*Note: Write your ticket, then delete the first paragraph. The problem probably starts in paragraph two. If you cannot find the problem in under 50 words, the problem is your writing, not the computer.*
