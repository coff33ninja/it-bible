## The "I Fixed It First" Disaster

---

**BEFORE YOU 'FIX' THE PROBLEM BEFORE CALLING IT...**

- You tried to fix it yourself. Now it is not only still broken, but it is also broken in a way that is harder to diagnose because I now have to undo whatever you did before I can even see the original problem.
- "I ran a registry cleaner" — you did not fix the issue. You deleted 400 critical system entries. The original problem is still there, now compounded by a lobotomised OS.
- "I uninstalled some programs I did not recognise" — you uninstalled the VPN client, the antivirus, and the print driver. The original issue was that your email signature was wrong. You have now created 3 new problems.

*Note: "I tried to fix it" is the most expensive preface in IT support. The diagnostic fee doubles when I have to reverse your 'help.'*

---

**BEFORE YOU WATCH A YOUTUBE VIDEO AND FOLLOW THE STEPS...**

- The YouTube video is called "How to Speed Up Windows 10." The steps include disabling services, deleting prefetch files, and modifying the registry. None of these apply to your specific issue.
- The video was made by a 19-year-old in his bedroom who has never managed a corporate network. His advice is not applicable to your enterprise environment.
- You followed the steps exactly. The steps were wrong. Your machine is now slower, less secure, and 3 critical services have been disabled. Congratulations.

*Note: YouTube is not a certification. A tutorial from a stranger is not IT support. Close the video and call the helpdesk.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK YOUR 'TECH SAVVY' FRIEND TO 'TAKE A LOOK'...**

- Your friend knows how to build a gaming PC. That does not mean they know how to configure a corporate VPN, troubleshoot a print server, or recover a corrupted Outlook profile.
- "He fixed it before" — he fixed it by reinstalling Windows. That is not "fixing." That is the nuclear option. He did not solve the problem. He nuked it from orbit and hoped it would not come back.
- Now your friend has uninstalled the company antivirus and installed a "lighter" one he found online. Your machine is no longer compliant with our security policy and will be quarantined from the network.

*Note: Your friend's "fix" just created a compliance violation. We now have to reimage your machine entirely. Thanks for the help.*

---

**BEFORE YOU REINSTALL WINDOWS 'TO FIX A SLOW BROWSER'...**

- The browser was slow because you had 47 extensions installed and 200 tabs open. Reinstalling the operating system fixes that, but at what cost? You just wiped every file, setting, and configuration that was not backed up.
- "I backed up my files first" — you backed up your Documents folder. You did not back up your Outlook signature, your saved Wi-Fi passwords, your browser bookmarks, or the configuration files for the 6 line-of-business applications you use daily.
- A slow browser is not an operating system problem. It is a "clean your browser" problem. You escalated this from a 5-minute fix to a 4-hour rebuild.

*Note: Reinstalling Windows because Chrome is slow is like demolishing your house because the sink is clogged.*

---

**BEFORE YOU 'GOOGLE THE ERROR MESSAGE' AND FOLLOW THE FIRST RESULT...**

- The first result on Google is a forum post from 2012 where someone suggested deleting a DLL file. That DLL file is used by 3 critical applications. You deleted it. Those applications no longer work.
- "The forum post said it worked for them" — it worked for them on their specific configuration, with their specific OS version, and their specific hardware. Yours is different. The result is different.
- Googling error messages is what IT does. The difference is we know which results are relevant, which are outdated, and which will destroy your machine. You do not have that filter.

*Note: Dr. Google is not a licensed IT practitioner. WebMD for computers has the same reliability as WebMD for humans: terrifying and usually wrong.*
