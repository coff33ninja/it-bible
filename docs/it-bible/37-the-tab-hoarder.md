## The Tab Hoarder

---

**BEFORE YOU KEEP 200 BROWSER TABS OPEN 'FOR REFERENCE'...**

- You have 200 tabs open across 4 browser windows. The browser is using 8GB of RAM. Your machine has 16GB total. Half of your system memory is dedicated to holding open pages you have not looked at in 3 weeks.
- "I will read them later" — you will not. The tabs have been open for so long that some of them have logged you out. The pinned tab from 2 months ago is a relic of a task you completed and forgot about.
- Each open tab consumes memory, CPU cycles, and your sanity. The browser is not a filing system. It is a temporary viewing window. Close the tabs. Bookmark the ones you need. Revisit them when you actually need them.

*Note: A browser with 200 tabs is not "organised." It is a digital hoarding situation that requires intervention.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE YOUR BROWSER AS A TO-DO LIST...**

- Your to-do list is a collection of 47 open tabs representing tasks you intend to complete. The tabs are not a task manager. They are an anxiety collection. Every open tab is a reminder of something you have not done.
- "I keep them open so I do not forget" — you will forget anyway. The tabs will blend into the noise of the other 153 tabs. The important task will be buried under cat videos and news articles.
- There are applications for task management. They are called "to-do list apps." They are designed for this exact purpose. Your browser tabs are not a productivity system. They are a symptom of avoidance.

*Note: Using browser tabs as a memory aid is like writing reminders on toilet paper. Ineffective and destined to be flushed.*

---

**BEFORE YOU COMPLAIN THAT YOUR BROWSER IS 'TOO SLOW'...**

- Your browser has 200 tabs open, 47 extensions installed, and a cache that has not been cleared since the previous administration. The browser is not slow. It is drowning.
- "I only have a few tabs open" — you have 4 browser windows with 50 tabs each. You have normalised the chaos. Your perception of "a few" is clinically detached from reality.
- The browser is using more memory than your operating system. Chrome's task manager shows that your tabs are consuming resources like a fleet of idling trucks. Close them.

*Note: Your browser is not slow. You have given it more work than it can handle. Close some tabs before blaming the software.*

---

**BEFORE YOU HAVE MULTIPLE BROWSERS OPEN WITH THE SAME TABS...**

- You have Chrome open with 80 tabs, Firefox open with 60 tabs, and Edge open with 40 tabs. All of them contain the same types of content: research, unfinished tasks, and YouTube videos.
- Each browser is independently consuming memory. You have tripled the resource usage for the same set of open tasks. This is not "organising by browser." This is madness.
- "I use different browsers for different things" — you use different browsers because you ran out of tab space in one and refused to close any tabs. That is not a workflow. That is an intervention waiting to happen.

*Note: Multiple browsers with duplicate categories of open tabs is not multitasking. It is computer-aided hoarding.*

---

**BEFORE YOU PIN EVERY WEBSITE YOU USE TO THE TASKBAR...**

- Your taskbar has 30 pinned applications. The icons have shrunk to the point where they are indistinguishable. You hover over each one to read the tooltip because you cannot tell Outlook from Excel anymore.
- "I pin everything so I can access it quickly" — you cannot access anything quickly because you have to visually search through a row of identical-looking icons every time.
- The taskbar is not a desktop. It is a launcher for frequently used applications. Not every application you have ever installed. Not every website you have ever visited. Just the ones you use daily.

*Note: A taskbar that requires a magnifying glass to navigate is not "efficient." It is a wall of noise with no signal.*
