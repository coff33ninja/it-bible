## The Escalation Abuser / CEO CC

---

**BEFORE YOU CC THE CEO ON A TIER 1 SUPPORT TICKET...**

- You submitted a ticket asking for a password reset and CC'd the CEO. The CEO does not care about your forgotten password. The CEO now has an email about your forgotten password. This is not the impression you think it is.
- CC'ing executives on routine IT issues does not make the issue resolve faster. It makes the IT team resent you and the executives question your competence.
- "I wanted to make sure it was taken seriously" — all tickets are taken seriously. Your attempt to bypass the queue by invoking executive authority has just landed your ticket in a special queue. The "reviewed by legal" queue.

*Note: CC'ing the CEO on a password reset ticket is the IT equivalent of calling the fire department because your toast is burnt.*

---

**BEFORE YOU USE 'URGENT' ON A NON-URGENT TICKET...**

- Your font is not printing correctly. That is not urgent. Urgent is "the server is on fire." Urgent is "the entire billing system is offline." Urgent is not "Word is showing the wrong margins."
- When every ticket is marked urgent, urgent loses all meaning. Your false urgency means that when there is an actual emergency, genuine urgent tickets get lost in the noise of your "my desktop wallpaper changed" false alarms.
- The system tracks how many of your tickets are actually urgent. The ratio is not in your favour. The data does not lie.

*Note: If everything is urgent, nothing is urgent. Your font issue will be handled in the order it was received, which is after the server fire.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SAY 'THIS IS A BOARD-LEVEL PRIORITY'...**

- "Board-level priority" is a phrase people use when they want their routine request fast-tracked. The board has never heard of you or your spreadsheet formatting issue.
- If the board is genuinely involved, I will know about it before you do. The board does not send intermediaries. The board calls directly.
- Invoking the board's name does not grant you executive privilege. It grants you a special flag on your account that reads "User claims board involvement. Usually untrue."

*Note: The board is not your personal IT escalation hotline. Do not use their name in vain.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SEND A TICKET TO 10 DIFFERENT PEOPLE AT ONCE...**

- Emailing 10 different IT staff members the same request does not create 10 parallel work streams. It creates 10 people who are all confused about who is handling it, resulting in either duplicate work or everyone assuming someone else will handle it.
- "I wanted to make sure someone saw it" — all 10 people saw it. None of them are responding because they are waiting for the other 9 to take ownership. You have effectively paralysed the entire support team.
- The support queue is a single, organised system. Sending emails to individual staff members bypasses that system and guarantees your request will be handled slower, not faster.

*Note: Spray-and-pray emailing to the entire IT department is not escalation. It is noise generation. Pick one person and wait.*

---

**BEFORE YOU SAY 'I NEED THIS BY YESTERDAY'...**

- If you needed it yesterday, you should have submitted it yesterday. The fact that you submitted it 5 minutes ago and are demanding yesterday-level turnaround is not a scheduling conflict on my end. It is a planning failure on yours.
- "I know it is last minute, but..." — if you know it is last minute, you had time to anticipate it. You chose not to. Your lack of planning does not constitute my emergency.
- Time machines do not exist in our software stack. I cannot backdate my work. I can only work forward from the moment you actually submitted the request.

*Note: A "needed by yesterday" deadline submitted today is called "a failure to plan." The IT department does not have a time machine.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ADD THE IT DIRECTOR TO A TICKET ABOUT A MINOR ISSUE...**

- Your mouse battery is low. You added the IT Director to the ticket. The IT Director does not change mouse batteries. The IT Director manages the team that changes mouse batteries. You have now escalated a battery replacement to executive leadership.
- Adding senior leadership to a low-level ticket does not make it more important. It makes you look like you cannot handle a mouse battery replacement without involving the chain of command.
- The IT Director's daily rate is approximately R12,000. They just spent 30 seconds reading your mouse battery ticket. That cost the company R83 of executive time for a problem that costs R5 to fix.

*Note: Low-level tickets with executive CCs are not impressive. They are embarrassing for everyone involved.*
