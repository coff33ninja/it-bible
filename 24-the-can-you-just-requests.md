## The "Can You Just..." Requests

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST MAKE IT FASTER?'...**

- "Can you just make it faster?" is the most technically illiterate question in IT. It assumes there is a "speed slider" somewhere in the settings that I have been hiding from you.
- I cannot make your 5-year-old laptop with a spinning hard drive run like a new M-series Mac. The laws of physics have already set the maximum speed of your hardware.
- "Can you just upgrade my RAM?" — that is not a "just." That is a hardware intervention. It costs money and takes time. Also, your laptop's RAM is soldered. So no.

*Note: "Make it faster" is not a request. It is a wish. I am a technician, not a genie.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST GIVE ME ADMIN RIGHTS?'...**

- Admin rights allow you to install any software, modify any system file, and completely destroy the OS in ways that require a full reimage to fix.
- "I need admin rights to install a PDF reader" — no, you do not. The company-approved PDF reader is already in the software centre. Click install. No admin rights required.
- "I will be careful" — everyone says that. Then they install a toolbar, a "free" screensaver, and a driver updater from a pop-up ad. Then they call me to fix the mess.

*Note: Admin rights are not a privilege. They are a liability waiver. Every admin user is one bad click away from a reimage.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST COPY MY COLLEAGUE'S SETUP?'...**

- Your colleague has a specific set of software, permissions, and configurations that were set up over months of troubleshooting. I cannot "just copy" a human's workflow.
- "We do the same job, why can't I have what they have?" — because you have different requirements, different access needs, and different software licenses.
- Also, your colleague's machine is a mess. They have 47 browser extensions, 3 expired antivirus trials, and a desktop cluttered with files named "final_v3_REAL(1).docx." You do not want their setup.

*Note: "Copy their setup" is not a technical request. It is a complaint about fairness dressed up as an IT issue.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST UNLOCK THIS WEBSITE?'...**

- The website is blocked by the firewall because it is classified as "gambling," "adult content," or "malware distribution." There is a reason it is blocked.
- "I need it for research" — you do not. You want to access it on company time. The firewall logs will confirm which category it falls under.
- Requesting an unblock requires manager approval. Your manager will be notified of the request. If the site is work-related, they will approve it. If it is not, they will not.

*Note: "Can you just unblock this?" is a request for me to bypass security policy. I will not. Ask your manager to submit the formal request.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST TAKE A QUICK LOOK?'...**

- "A quick look" is never quick. It is a 45-minute investigation that starts with "I will just check one thing" and ends with me rebuilding your Outlook profile, resetting your browser, and updating 14 drivers.
- "A quick look" is how unpaid overtime is born. It is how a 5-minute fix becomes a 2-hour pro-bono support session.
- If it needs looking at, submit a ticket. If it is urgent, call. If it is neither, wait. "A quick look" is not a valid support channel.

*Note: "Can you just look?" is the precursor to "While you are here..." which is the precursor to me regretting my life choices.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST FIX IT OVER THE PHONE?'...**

- I cannot see your screen over the phone. I cannot click buttons for you over the phone. I cannot run diagnostics over the phone.
- "I will describe what is happening" — your description will be "there is an error." That is not a description. That is a state of being.
- Remote support requires a remote connection or a ticket with detailed information. Pick one. "Just fix it" is not actionable.

*Note: IT support over the phone is limited to "turn it off and on again." If you need more than that, I need access to your machine.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST MAKE AN EXCEPTION?'...**

- An exception for you means an exception for everyone. Once I bend the rule for one person, I cannot enforce it for anyone else.
- "Just this once" — and next week you will ask again. And the week after that. And then everyone in your department will ask for the same exception.
- Security policies exist because of the aggregate behaviour of everyone before you who asked for "just one exception" and then caused a breach.

*Note: "Just one exception" is how security policies die. The answer is no. It will always be no.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK 'CAN YOU JUST...' ANYTHING...**

- If the request starts with "Can you just," it is probably more complicated than you think.
- If it were a "just," you would have done it yourself.
- "Just" is the most dangerous word in IT support. It minimises the effort, trivialises the complexity, and guarantees the requester has no idea what they are asking for.

*Note: Remove "just" from your vocabulary when talking to IT. Say what you need, let me decide how complicated it is.*
