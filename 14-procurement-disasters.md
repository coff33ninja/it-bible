## Procurement Disasters

---

**BEFORE YOU BUY A LAPTOP FROM A 'GREAT DEAL' ONLINE...**

- If the price is too good to be true, the laptop is either stolen, refurbished with non-genuine parts, or a counterfeit running a skin over Android.
- "Intel Core i7" means nothing when it is a 12-year-old low-voltage mobile chip from a scrapped lineup.
- No, you cannot return it because "it's slow." You bought a device with 4GB of RAM and eMMC storage in 2026. What precisely did you expect?

*Note: The cheapest listing on PriceCheck is not a "hack." It is a trap.*

---

**BEFORE YOU ORDER A CHARGER FROM A SHADY WEBSITE...**

- That R79 "universal laptop charger" will absolutely charge your laptop. Once. Then it will either melt, catch fire, or deliver a dirty voltage spike that kills the charging IC permanently.
- A genuine charger costs what it costs because it contains proper voltage regulation, shielding, and safety certifications. The knock-off contains hopes, dreams, and loose solder joints.
- "It fits the port" is not the same as "it is electrically compatible with this device."

*Note: Saving R200 on a charger today costs you R8,000 for a new motherboard tomorrow. Your choice.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BUY A 'SERVER-GRADE' DESKTOP PC FROM A LIQUIDATION SALE...**

- That enterprise workstation you picked up for a "steal" is a Dell Precision from 2016 with a Xeon CPU that draws 165W at idle and sounds like a hair dryer.
- It came with Windows 7. Upgrading it to Windows 11 requires bypassing every hardware guard Microsoft designed. It will not be stable.
- The previous company decommissioned it for a reason. That reason was not "it was too powerful."

*Note: Ex-corporate hardware is cheap for a reason. That reason is electricity bills and fan noise that will make your home office uninhabitable.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BUY A MONITOR WITHOUT CHECKING THE INPUTS...**

- You just bought a gorgeous 4K monitor with no HDMI ports. Only VGA. In 2026.
- "I thought all monitors had HDMI" is an expensive assumption.
- Yes, we have an adapter. No, the adapter will not make it look good. VGA on a 4K panel looks like a impressionist painting of a spreadsheet.

*Note: Check the I/O panel before you click "Buy." We cannot return opened electronics for "my mistake."*

---

**BEFORE YOU ASK WHY THE 'INDUSTRIAL' ROUTER WON'T WORK AT HOME...**

- That enterprise-grade Cisco/Mikrotik router you bought second-hand was designed for a data center with a dedicated network engineer on staff.
- The configuration interface is a command line with no hand-holding. There is no "wizard" mode. There is no "plug and play."
- "Can't you just set it up quickly?" — I can, but the 47 firewall rules, VLAN segmentation, and BGP routing you don't need will still be running in the background consuming electricity.

*Note: Buying enterprise gear because it was "cheaper than a Asus router" is like buying a freight truck because it was cheaper than a hatchback. It will not fit in your garage.*

---

**BEFORE YOU IMPORT ELECTRONICS WITHOUT CHECKING THE PLUG...**

- That American-specification server you imported runs on 110V. Our walls deliver 230V. Plugging it in directly will result in an immediate and spectacular fireworks display.
- A step-down transformer is heavy, expensive, and generates heat. It is not a "simple adapter."
- No amount of praying to the electrical gods will make 230V equal 110V. Physics does not negotiate.

*Note: South Africa uses 230V with a Type M plug. Check the voltage rating before you click "Ship to South Africa."*

---

**BEFORE YOU BUY A 'BUNDLE' DEAL WITH UNMATCHED COMPONENTS...**

- "Intel motherboard with AMD CPU" — these are not compatible. They have never been compatible. They will never be compatible.
- The pins are different. The sockets are different. The electrical signalling is different. It is not a "minor incompatibility."
- "But the seller said they work together" — the seller is a liar who has already received your money and blocked your number.

*Note: CPU and motherboard compatibility is not a matter of opinion. It is a matter of physics. Do not trust a listing that contradicts physics.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BUY A PRINTER BASED SOLELY ON THE PRICE OF THE PRINTER...**

- Printers are not sold at a loss out of generosity. They are sold at a loss because the manufacturer intends to recoup the margin through ink cartridge sales.
- That R399 printer will consume R2,000 worth of ink in the first year. The cartridges will have half the ink volume of the standard size and cost the same.
- "But it's cheap to buy" — yes, and a free puppy is also cheap until you realize the cost of feeding it.

*Note: A cheap printer is the most expensive printer you can buy. Do the math on ink cost per page before purchasing.*

---

**BEFORE YOU BUY A 'GAMING' LAPTOP AS A "PORTABLE WORKSTATION"...**

- Gaming laptops are not portable workstations. They are desktop replacements with a battery that lasts 45 minutes.
- The "gaming" tax means you paid extra for RGB lighting, a chassis that looks like a transformer, and fans loud enough to drown out a lawnmower.
- For the same price, a business-class Ultrabook would be lighter, quieter, faster at productivity tasks, and last 8 hours on battery.

*Note: Buying a gaming laptop for Excel and email is like buying a Ferrari to drive to the post office. Impressive, but deeply impractical.*
